# TX-11: August Pfluger — Financial Disclosure Summary (2026 Cycle)

August Pfluger's 2026-cycle money runs through four committees, and three of them matter: his principal campaign committee (**August Pfluger for Congress**, C00719294), a joint fundraising committee that does the real heavy lifting (**Pfluger Victory Fund**, C00753913), and his leadership PAC (**Raptor PAC**, C00749481). A fourth, much smaller joint fundraising vehicle (**PFriends of Pfluger**, C00840033) is largely a 2024-cycle artifact that shows almost no 2026 activity. Across all four, itemized donor receipts total **$5,317,758.43** for the 2026 cycle — about 73% from individuals ($3,882,108.43) and 27% from PACs and other committees ($1,435,650.00). Inter-committee transfers among Pfluger's own entities are excluded from that figure, so the JFC's money isn't double-counted.

The principal campaign raised **$642,408.43** directly from donors this cycle — a modest sum on its own. The real engine is the Pfluger Victory Fund, which took in **$4,617,350.00** in large checks and redistributed the proceeds: **$2,090,787.64** back to the campaign, **$922,789.74** to Raptor PAC, **$638,886.02** onward to the NRCC, and **$130,655.02** to the House Conservatives Fund, a Republican leadership-aligned PAC. Raptor PAC, in turn, functions as a political gift shop, writing checks of $1,000–$14,000 to roughly 235 other Republican candidate committees around the country.

## Major Spending

Combined disbursements across all four committees total **$8,081,363.33**, but $3,841,573.54 of that (42 transactions) is Transfers — the JFC, campaign, and PAC moving money among Pfluger's own entities and to allied party committees, not spending on the campaign itself. Setting transfers aside, actual operating spend is **$4,239,789.79**, broken out by FEC category:

- **Administrative, salary, and overhead — $1,779,330.42** (656 items). Top named vendors: American Express ($914,557.70, mostly a card-processor pass-through, see below), Citicard ($195,607.82), fundraiser Caroline Brennan ($178,839.27), CFS Compliance ($142,662.61), Grace Dunham ($124,685.32), and Gusto payroll ($110,222.35).
- **Political contributions — $1,067,500.00** (459 items). This is Raptor PAC's chit-building at work: 459 checks of $1,000–$14,000 spread across roughly 235 other Republican candidate committees and PACs — everything from Ashley Hinson for Congress ($14,000) down to freshman recruits and open-seat hopefuls like Matt Van Epps, Clay Fuller, and Gabe Evans ($10,000 each).
- **Solicitation and fundraising expenses — $921,673.10** (148 items), led by Hooks Solutions LLC ($102,997.34) and NGHMH LLC ($44,101.06).
- **Advertising — just $133,299.71** (13 items), almost entirely through Targeted Victory (part of its $414,503.69 total across categories, mostly digital fundraising infrastructure) and Targeted Creative Communications ($46,317.76). Voter-facing persuasion spending is strikingly thin relative to everything else moving through these books.
- **Campaign events — $94,880.05** (30 items); **campaign materials — $70,477.78** (53 items); **travel — $70,268.38** (50 items); **polling — $38,375.39** (4 items); **donations — $35,659.98** (18 items); **refunds of contributions — $26,224.98** (199 items, mostly small excess-contribution returns); and a single **$2,100 uncategorized** in-kind catering entry.

American Express, the single biggest named vendor at $914,557.70, is mostly not a black box. The top vendors inside those lump payments read like a donor-cultivation circuit: **American Airlines ($107,138.62)**; a Washington fine-dining run of **The Capital Grille ($36,819.37), Del Frisco's Double Eagle Steakhouse ($27,582.00), Oceanaire ($21,452.47), Mi Vida ($17,158.55), and Fiola Mare ($17,041.10)** — well over $100K in upscale D.C. restaurants; and travel/lodging spend including Hilton DC ($19,770.61) and WinRed's technical-services fee ($30,289.84).

Beyond the card breakdown, the Pfluger Victory Fund also cut direct, non-memo checks to a cluster of destination venues in 2025: **Stein Eriksen Lodge**, a Deer Valley, Utah ski resort, received $578.72 for lodging in March plus $43,959.03 for "facility rental and catering" in August (two separate filings under slightly different spellings of the same venue); the **St. Regis Deer Valley Resort**, also in Park City, appears separately for $18,735.80 in the card-breakdown data — putting total Deer Valley/Park City spending north of $63,000. **St. Joe Resort and Operations LLC**, which operates Gulf Coast Florida beach resorts, billed $37,819.04 for "facility rental & catering" in October 2025. And **Baltusrol Golf Club**, the historic New Jersey country club, collected roughly $15,900 across four separate 2025 events (event tickets, catering, and two facility rentals). None of this shows up under "Uncategorized" — it's cleanly itemized, just spread across ski, beach, and golf venues far from San Angelo.

## Takeaways

1. **This is a leadership-building operation, not a persuasion campaign.** Just $133,299.71 in advertising all cycle, against $1,067,500.00 in political contributions to other Republicans and $638,886.02 transferred to the NRCC. Pfluger — chair of the Republican Study Committee — is spending like a member cultivating chits across the conference rather than one bracing for a competitive race, even in a cycle when his redrawn district newly reaches into Austin's northern suburbs.

2. **A single Midland oilman matches the entire direct-donor campaign.** Syed Javaid Anwar's $642,100 (routed through the Pfluger Victory Fund) is equal to the principal campaign raised from every donor combined ($642,408.43, just $300 shy). When one donor's giving approaches your entire direct fundraising base, that relationship, not the base, is the financial foundation.

3. **The funding isn't limited to Pfluger's own JFC.** A second, externally-run joint fundraising committee — the American Battleground Fund — passed at least $91,128.90 in earmarked contributions to the campaign this cycle. That committee isn't collected here, so its own donor list (who actually wrote the underlying checks) is invisible in this report; see "Suggested Committees" below.

4. **The donor base and the disclosed trades point the same direction, with real nuance in whose money it is.** The top-10 donor list is dominated by Permian Basin oil-and-gas executives. Pfluger's own House Ethics filings show a burst of activity in March 2026: six new **$15,001–$50,000 purchases** — Amerco/U-Haul, Berkshire Hathaway, Dorchester Minerals, Enterprise Products Partners, Kimbell Royalty Partners, and Viper Energy — all filed under a plain "Investment" account with no spousal or dependent-child owner code, meaning these are attributable to Pfluger himself. Three of the six (Dorchester Minerals, Enterprise Products Partners, Viper Energy) are oil-and-gas midstream or royalty names, filed by a member who sits on the House Energy and Commerce Committee. The sale-side activity disclosed across seven earlier 2025–2026 filings (SiriusXM, Liberty Media, Warner Bros. Discovery, Walt Disney, Fidelity National Financial, each $1,001–$15,000) is a mixed bag that shouldn't be read as a pattern of Pfluger's own active trading: several are owned by his spouse ("SP") or a dependent child ("DC"), and two of the SiriusXM/Liberty Media entries are coded "Exchange," not "Sale" — non-discretionary share reclassifications from Liberty Media's corporate spin-offs, not trades anyone chose to make. Dollar figures throughout are disclosure ranges, not exact amounts.

5. **The annual Financial Disclosure Statement for 2025 isn't in this dataset — and may not be public yet.** The `house-ethics/` directory holds seven Periodic Transaction Reports and one document that isn't a PTR at all: an extension request, filed 2026-04-14, pushing the due date for Pfluger's 2025 annual Financial Disclosure Statement (the broader income/asset/liability report, distinct from PTRs) from 2026-05-15 to 2026-08-13 — two days before this report was generated. That statement doesn't appear to have been collected yet, so this report's picture of Pfluger's broader financial holdings is necessarily limited to what PTRs disclose (individual transactions), not his full asset and liability picture.

## Suggested Committees for Further Investigation

Everything Pfluger directly controls — principal committee, leadership PAC, and his own JFC — is already collected and analyzed above. Three uncollected committees stand out from data already visible in what's on disk:

- **American Battleground Fund [C00857649]** — a national, multi-candidate joint fundraising committee that earmarked $91,128.90 to Pfluger's principal campaign this cycle (visible directly in the campaign's own Schedule A, under "Transfers from authorized committees" — see Key Donors above). This is the strongest lead: it's real inbound donor money not reflected in the Key Donors table, and a full `fec-api-client.rb --download --committee-id C00857649` would surface the underlying individual/PAC donors behind that $91K.
- **NRCC [C00075820]** — received $638,886.02 from the Pfluger Victory Fund this cycle (plus smaller amounts via other committees, for $874,386.02 combined across all transfer-recipient records). It's the national party's House campaign arm — a full itemized download would pull in an enormous, mostly-unrelated dataset. If pursued at all, `fec-api-client.rb --with-affiliated` for totals-only context is the better fit, not a full itemized `--download`.
- **House Conservatives Fund [C00326439]** — received $130,655.02, entirely from the Pfluger Victory Fund. It's a Republican leadership PAC; a deliberate `fec-api-client.rb --download --committee-id C00326439` would show what share of its budget comes from Pfluger's network and where that money then goes.

The roughly 235 individual candidate committees receiving $1,000–$14,000 checks from Raptor PAC are visible in the transfer-recipient list and don't individually warrant collection — the pattern (breadth of giving across the conference) is itself the finding, and it's already fully captured in the Political Contributions category above.
