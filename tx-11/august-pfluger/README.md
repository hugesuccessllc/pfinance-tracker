# TX-11: August Pfluger — Financial Disclosure Summary (2026 Cycle)

Rep. August Pfluger (R) runs one of the more elaborate small-district money operations in Texas. Four committees he controls report activity in the 2025–2026 cycle:

- his campaign, **August Pfluger for Congress** (C00719294);
- a joint fundraising committee (JFC), **Pfluger Victory Fund** (C00753913; some filings call it "Pfluger Victory Committee"). A JFC collects big checks and splits them among its participants;
- a leadership PAC, **Raptor PAC** (C00749481);
- a nearly dormant committee, **Pfriends of Pfluger** (C00840033). Its only activity this cycle was a $5,000 check from E-PAC in July 2025, refunded in August.

Together they took in about **$6.06 million in contributions** from Jan. 1, 2025 through Sept. 30, 2026 (through Aug. 31 for Raptor PAC). More than $4.9 million of that came in through the JFC. The campaign ended the period with **$2.25 million cash on hand**, slightly less than the $2.32 million it started with: it spent a bit more than it raised.

TX-11 is anchored in Midland, Odessa and San Angelo. The redrawn map also adds a strip of North Austin, most of Pflugerville, and parts of Round Rock, Cedar Park and Leander.

## Takeaways

**1. One Midland oilman supplied about one in every eight dollars the JFC raised.** Syed Javaid Anwar of Midland Energy gave the JFC two checks: $332,100 in February 2025 and $310,000 in March 2026. Together they equal about 13% of everything the JFC raised in the cycle. Most of that money could not have legally gone to Pfluger's campaign, which can take only $3,500 per election from one person. The timing suggests where it went. The day after the March 2026 check arrived, the JFC sent the NRCC two transfers of $132,900 and $132,800. Those are the size of the higher limits for donations to the NRCC's special-purpose accounts. The JFC's own allocation records would be needed to confirm the link. Either way, it shows that Pfluger's operation works as a channel for Permian Basin money to the national party as much as a campaign treasury.

**2. Pfluger's money comes from the Permian and San Angelo, not from the parts of the district added by redistricting.** Of the JFC's $3.69 million in itemized individual contributions, 50.5% came from Midland addresses and another 17.4% from San Angelo. Texas as a whole accounts for 89%. The Austin-area communities the new map added (North Austin, Pflugerville, Round Rock, Cedar Park and Leander) gave just $17,500, under half a percent. They are a sizable share of the new district's voters, and so far they barely appear in his donor rolls.

**3. Some top donors also appear, by name, in his personal investments.** His annual House disclosure for calendar 2025, filed Aug. 12, 2026, lists a new holding: **Pfluger Energy Investments, LLC**. Through it he owns interests in three ventures:
- "Double Eagle V," a Fort Worth company that "acquires oil and gas leases," valued at $100,001–$250,000;
- "Trace," a Houston natural-gas pipeline builder, valued at $100,001–$250,000;
- "Tumbleweed V," a Fort Worth mineral-rights buyer, valued at $15,001–$50,000.

None of these appears on his previous year's report, and the 2025 report lists no matching purchase. A separate family LLC, PMP Spirits, holds an interest in "Double Eagle Spirits Holdco LLC." Meanwhile, the two co-CEOs of Fort Worth's Double Eagle Energy, Cody Campbell and John Sellers, gave his JFC $100,000 together with Sellers's wife Tracy. The filing does not say who sponsors "Double Eagle V," so the link is a matching name and city, not a confirmed business relationship. It is worth asking him directly.

The pattern continues in his stock trades. In March 2026 he bought six stocks worth $15,001–$50,000 each. Four of them are oil-and-gas royalty, mineral or pipeline companies: Dorchester Minerals, Kimbell Royalty Partners, Viper Energy and Enterprise Products Partners. That matters for a member who, outside these filings, is known to sit on the House Energy and Commerce Committee. All four of his 2026 trade reports were filed within the STOCK Act's deadlines.

**4. Most of the money goes to the party and to colleagues.** About $2.1 million went to the NRCC, the House Conservatives Fund, and 253 other Republican candidates and committees. That is roughly a third of everything his committees spent outside his own network, and more than the campaign spent on ads, polling and staff combined. Raptor PAC is almost entirely a distribution operation: 455 checks to candidates, against about $184,000 in its own operating costs, a good share of which went to compliance fees, catering, printing and donor gifts. A member in a district he has won with over 75% of the vote has room to do this. The spending also explains why his campaign spent slightly more than it raised.

**5. Late in 2026, the campaign started spending like a contested race.** For most of the cycle it spent mainly on fundraising and on party dues. That changed in the third quarter of 2026, when it paid for survey research ($82,500), yard signs and installation, printing, and newspaper ads in rural counties. With a Democratic challenger on the November ballot for the first time since 2020, the campaign is now paying for voter outreach as well as fundraising.

**Where the data-integrity checks changed findings:**
- **Tool bug fixed this session.** The efile gap-fill counted line-11AI receipts only when `entity_type` was `IND`. - **A $50,000 "transfer" that went to the NRCC.** The campaign reported a $50,000 "transfer to other authorized committees" that actually went to the NRCC. It is counted with the NRCC.
- **Cycle-integrity warning.** The tool flagged 558 rows where `two_year_transaction_period` and `fec_election_year` disagree. Almost all are prior-cycle rows outside this report's scope. **Prompt history (v8):** Several `$CANDIDATE_DIR/reports/` write-ups regenerated in the same session as a data refresh or a tooling fix (a keyword-scan expansion, an efile-gap bug fix) kept spending a chunk of the new report explaining how the new numbers differed from the old ones — "the earlier version found $X, this version found $Y" — right in the body a reader would see. That's a changelog, not a report, and it's exactly the instinct "A standing rule: reports are not diffs" (above, under "Step 2: Analyze") now exists to head off. v8 doesn't change anything about how `analyze-candidate.rb` is run; it adds one instruction to this prompt's own Methodology section (below) making that standing rule concrete for this specific template, so a fresh session re-running this prompt for a new cycle — or a user regenerating an existing report after a fix — doesn't have to be told twice.

**Prompt history (v7):** A v6 run on Claire Reynolds's committee (TX-11, C00929711) reported her donor/spending activity as ending 2026-03-31, silently missing an entire second quarter (April–June 2026) that the campaign had already filed. The cause: `analyze-candidate.rb` only ever read `schedule_a-*.csv`/`schedule_b-*.csv` — fec.gov's "processed" bulk exports — and those can lag well behind what's actually been filed, sometimes by months, with no warning that they're stale. The raw `efile-*.csv` data sitting in the same directory (from clicking "Raw data" during Step 1's manual export, or from `fec-api-client.rb`'s automated download) already had the missing quarter; the tool just never looked at it. `analyze-candidate.rb` now reads `efile-*.csv` itself — but only ever for the narrow slice of rows dated strictly after whatever date the processed schedule_a/schedule_b export already covers, and only once per committee/schedule. It never touches or re-derives anything for a period the processed files already cover, which is what keeps this safe against the double-counting risk that kept efile out of scope entirely before (v6 and earlier told this prompt to never glob `efile-*.csv` in at all — see the v6 note below for why that caution existed, and gotcha 8 in `tooling/analyze-candidate.rb`'s header for the amendment-handling and name-casing details that make gap-filling correct rather than just plausible-looking). Nothing changed in this prompt's own instructions to make this happen — v7 exists only to record the incident and point at the tool as the actual fix, per this repo's standing rule that data-integrity logic lives in `analyze-candidate.rb`'s header, not duplicated here. One thing this prompt now does ask for explicitly: if the tool's output includes an `EFILE COVERAGE WARNING` banner, say so in the Methodology section (see the updated Methodology instructions below) rather than letting it pass unremarked — the whole point of surfacing it is so a reader can tell the difference between "no more recent filings exist" and "more recent filings exist and are already reflected below."

**Prompt history (v6):** v5 restricted this prompt to the principal committee **only**, even if other committees (a JFC, a leadership PAC) were already sitting collected in `$CANDIDATE_DIR/fec/`. That was overcorrection: the actual invariant Step 2 needs to hold is "never download, itemize, or otherwise collect anything itself" — not "only ever look at one committee." A user (or an LLM doing Step 1 as its own deliberate task) might already know a candidate's JFC matters and go collect it manually before ever running this prompt — say, because they read a prior "Suggested Committees" note, or just already knew about Raptor PAC and Pfluger Victory Committee and grabbed them alongside the principal committee from the start. Refusing to use data that's already sitting on disk, correctly collected, would be perverse. v6 analyzes **everything already collected** under `$CANDIDATE_DIR/fec/` — one committee or several — and reserves "Suggested Committees for Further Investigation" for naming committees that are visible but *not yet* collected, exactly as before.

**Prompt history (v5):** v4 still had this prompt run `fec-api-client.rb --download` itself, right before the analysis step, on the theory that a "principal committee only" download was narrow enough to be safe. In practice that still meant an analysis prompt could reach for the network and decide, on its own, that data needed fetching — the same instinct that led earlier versions to auto-chase linked/affiliated committees. v5 removes the download command from this prompt entirely. Collection (Step 1) and analysis (Step 2) are now strictly separate: if the required `fec/` data isn't already on disk when this prompt runs, **stop and say so** — tell the user which committee ID(s) to collect and point them at "Step 1: Collect Your Data" above — rather than fetching it yourself. This also makes the iterative workflow explicit: fast-pass on just the principal committee, read what it suggests investigating, go collect those committees yourself (Step 1 again, deliberately), then re-run this same analysis prompt against the now-larger `fec/` directory.

**Prompt history (v4):** v3 had this prompt download the principal committee's data plus, via a `--with-affiliated` flag, another committee auto-discovered by name search (and before that, a `--with-linked` flag that recursively crawled every committee ever referenced in a Schedule B transfer). Both put the *tool* in charge of deciding which other committees mattered — which either missed the committee that actually carried most of a candidate's money (a JFC's name search can fail; see `tooling/fec-api-client.rb`'s header) or, worse, pulled in large, unrelated committees (a party committee, another candidate's committee) that happened to receive a transfer. v4 scoped this prompt to the principal committee **only**, with `analyze-candidate.rb`'s own Schedule B data (a `recipient_committee_id` for any transfer recipient that's itself a committee — zero extra API calls) feeding a "Suggested Committees for Further Investigation" output section instead of an automatic download.

**Prompt history:** the pilot run of this prompt (TX-11/August-Pfluger) shipped a summary with a "Correction (post-publication review)" section — it took a second pass, prompted by a human asking pointed questions, to catch a couple of data-integrity bugs after the fact. Both are now fixed in [`/tooling/analyze-candidate.rb`](tooling/analyze-candidate.rb) and documented in its header comments, not repeated here — see the note below on why. This prompt tells the model to read and reuse that tool up front, specifically so a fresh session doesn't rediscover the same bugs before it can trust its own numbers. A "Correction" section in the output is a sign this prompt or the tool needs another pass, not an acceptable steady state.

**Tooling requirements:** Any tooling written to perform this analysis must be written in Ruby, using the version pinned in [`.ruby-version`](.ruby-version). If a chore is likely to come up more than once, write it as Ruby in `/tooling` from the start rather than reaching for a quick non-Ruby script now and meaning to port it later; that "temporary" script is exactly how a second, un-versioned, un-gemfiled toolchain accretes alongside the real one. Gems should be managed normally with Bundler and a `Gemfile`, so the repo remains portable and reproducible for anyone with `rbenv` and `bundler` installed. **Before writing anything new, check whether [`/tooling/analyze-candidate.rb`](tooling/analyze-candidate.rb) already exists and covers this candidate's data** (`ruby tooling/analyze-candidate.rb --help` shows its interface — run as plain `ruby`, not `bundle exec ruby`; see "Running the tool" under Step 2 below for why).
