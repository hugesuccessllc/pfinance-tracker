# Following One Donor's Money: Syed Javaid Anwar and the Pfluger Victory Fund

**District:** TX-11 · **Candidate:** August Pfluger · **Cycle:** 2026 (2025–2026)

## Summary

Syed Javaid Anwar, president and CEO of Midland Energy, Inc. of Midland, Texas, gave the Pfluger Victory Fund **$642,100** in the 2026 cycle in two checks. He is the joint fundraising committee's largest donor by a wide margin. His two contributions are the only receipts of $100,000 or more the committee records in the cycle.

Of that $642,100, **$12,000 (1.9%) reaches a committee August Pfluger controls.** Only $7,000 of it can be spent on his reelection campaign. That's the legal maximum an individual may give a House candidate across a cycle's two elections, and it all comes from the first check, in February 2025. By the time the second check is written thirteen months later, there is no room left, and none of it goes to the campaign.

The second check is unusually easy to follow. Anwar writes the joint fundraising committee a check for $310,000 on March 16, 2026. The next day, the committee transfers exactly $310,000 to the NRCC, the House Republicans' national campaign committee. The transfer comes in three pieces that match the per-donor ceilings for a national party committee's three accounts almost to the dollar.

That NRCC money mostly can't fund a House race:
- **Restricted:** $531,500 across both checks lands in accounts a national party committee may use only for a headquarters building or for legal and recount proceedings, not for candidates. That's 86% of the Anwar money bound for the NRCC, and 83% of everything he gave.
- **Unrestricted:** only the smaller main-account slice is party money that could plausibly reach a campaign, and this repository has no NRCC filings to confirm whether it does.

## The two checks

Both are itemized receipts to the Pfluger Victory Fund (C00753913), neither of them memo entries. Neither is refunded.

A joint fundraising committee can't keep what it raises. It splits every check among its participants according to the formula in its joint fundraising agreement. Each participant that receives a slice must list the original donor on its own Schedule A as a memo entry. That disclosure requirement is what makes the money followable.

## What reaches committees August Pfluger controls

There are three attribution rows. All trace to the February 2025 check, and all come with transfers the Pfluger Victory Fund sends on 2025-02-26.

| Landed at | Amount | Election | Via transfer | Attribution row |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| August Pfluger for Congress (C00719294) | $3,500.00 | Primary 2026 | 2025-02-26 | [schedule_a:41772](../fec/C00719294/schedule_a-2026-10-18T10_20_55.csv#L41772) |
| August Pfluger for Congress (C00719294) | $3,500.00 | General 2026 | 2025-02-26 | [schedule_a:41776](../fec/C00719294/schedule_a-2026-10-18T10_20_55.csv#L41776) |
| Raptor PAC (C00749481) | $5,000.00 | Primary 2026 | 2025-02-26 | [schedule_a:905](../fec/C00749481/schedule_a-2026-10-18T10_25_33.csv#L905) |
| **Total** | **$12,000.00** | | | |

The $7,000 to the campaign is the ceiling, not a coincidence. An individual may give a House candidate $3,500 per election, and the primary and general count separately. Anwar hits both caps with a single February 2025 check, nearly a year before the primary.

Raptor PAC, which received the $5,000, is Pfluger's leadership PAC. A leadership PAC can't spend on its own sponsor's race; it exists to give to other candidates. That money is real, and it buys influence, but it can't pay for a Pfluger campaign ad. Raptor PAC's own 2026 Schedule B shows this isn't theoretical. This cycle it makes 459 payments totaling $923,343.81 to 255 distinct recipient committees, almost all of them other Republican House campaigns, mostly in checks of $1,000 and $5,000. The recipients include Ashley Hinson, Miller-Meeks, Kean, Celeste, Alfonso, Huizenga and dozens more. Unlike the NRCC money below, this $5,000 really does end up spread across other GOP races.

**The March 2026 check produces no attribution rows at either committee.** Anwar is already maxed out to the campaign. Raptor PAC's limit is $5,000 per *calendar* year, so he has 2026 room there, but none of the March check is attributed to it.

## The March 2026 check: a next-day pass-through to the NRCC

The Pfluger Victory Fund's disbursement records show three transfers to the NRCC dated **2026-03-17**, one day after Anwar's $310,000 check:

| Date | Amount | Recipient | What that number is | Source row |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| 2026-03-17 | $44,300.00 | NRCC (C00075820) | Individual limit to a national party committee's main account, per calendar year | [schedule_b:1149](../fec/C00753913/schedule_b-2026-10-18T10_29_07.csv#L1149) |
| 2026-03-17 | $132,900.00 | NRCC (C00075820) | Individual limit to one additional national party account, per calendar year | [schedule_b:1167](../fec/C00753913/schedule_b-2026-10-18T10_29_07.csv#L1167) |
| 2026-03-17 | $132,800.00 | NRCC (C00075820) | Same limit, $100 short | [schedule_b:1166](../fec/C00753913/schedule_b-2026-10-18T10_29_07.csv#L1166) |
| | **$310,000.00** | | Exactly Anwar's check | |

Four things make this a pass-through rather than a coincidence:

1. **The sum is exact.** $44,300 + $132,900 + $132,800 = $310,000.00.
2. **The timing is next-day.** The check is dated 2026-03-16 and the transfers 2026-03-17.
3. **The tranches are limit-shaped.** National party committees may keep separate accounts for headquarters buildings and for recounts and legal proceedings. Each has its own per-donor annual cap, $132,900 in 2025–2026, on top of the $44,300 main-account limit. Three tranches, three accounts, three ceilings.
4. **Pooled transfers don't look like this.** Every other NRCC transfer the fund makes this cycle is a messy aggregate: $72,658.91, $94,121.72, $115,120.16, $15,544.75, $12,332.10, $11,035.13, $7,092.53, $980.72, and $9,177.50 in the third quarter of 2026. Round numbers sitting exactly on legal caps are the signature of one donor's check being split by formula, not many donors' money being swept together.

In effect, Anwar's $310,000 is a contribution to the national House campaign arm that August Pfluger's fundraising vehicle happens to solicit. Even "campaign arm" is generous for most of it:
- **The $44,300 main-account tranche** is unrestricted party money, the kind that can fund ads, coordinated spending or independent expenditures in a House race.
- **The two additional-account tranches** are not. FEC rules restrict a national party committee's additional accounts to its headquarters building fund and its legal/recount fund. That's **$265,700, or 86% of this single check**, legally barred from ever reaching a candidate.

## Reconstructing the February 2025 check

The $310,000 check can be read directly off the fund's records. The $332,100 check can't, because the 2025-02-26 transfers that carried it pooled it with other donors' money. Direct attribution establishes $12,000. The remaining $320,100 breaks down neatly against the 2025 ceilings, but that is arithmetic reconstruction, not disclosure:

| Slice | Amount | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| August Pfluger for Congress | $7,000.00 | Confirmed by attribution rows |
| Raptor PAC | $5,000.00 | Confirmed by attribution row |
| NRCC, main account | $44,300.00 | Inferred: 2025 annual individual limit |
| NRCC, additional account | $132,900.00 | Inferred: 2025 annual limit |
| NRCC, additional account | $132,900.00 | Inferred: 2025 annual limit |
| Unaccounted | $10,000.00 | Consistent with $5,000 a year to the House Conservatives Fund across two calendar years |
| **Total** | **$332,100.00** | |

The NRCC portion is the whole reason the check is the size it is. $44,300 + $132,900 + $132,900 = $310,100, a single donor's maximum annual giving to one national party committee. Add the $7,000 campaign maximum, the $5,000 leadership PAC maximum and $10,000 more, and you land on $332,100 exactly. Treat the last four rows as a plausible fit rather than a finding.

The same account rules apply here as to the March check. Of this reconstructed $310,100, only $44,300 is unrestricted party money. The remaining $265,800 is restricted to the NRCC's building and legal funds.

## Where the whole $642,100 lands, roughly

Putting the confirmed, traced and reconstructed pieces together. This is a summary of everything above, not a new calculation:

| Destination | Amount | Share | Can it fund a House race? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Pfluger's own campaign | $7,000 | ~1% | Yes: his race specifically |
| Raptor PAC → ~250 other GOP campaigns | $5,000 | ~1% | Yes: confirmed, spread widely |
| NRCC main account (both checks) | $88,600 | ~14% | Plausibly; no NRCC filings here to confirm |
| NRCC building/legal accounts (both checks) | $531,500 | ~83% | **No:** restricted by law to real estate and litigation |
| Unaccounted | $10,000 | ~2% | Unknown |

The headline comparison here sets Anwar's giving against every direct donor to the campaign. Once his check is split by formula and by restricted-account rules, it barely touches the campaign at all. Most of it isn't spread into other candidates' races either. It funds the national party's own infrastructure.

## Caveats

- **Only $12,000 of the $642,100 is traced by disclosure.** The rest is followed to a set of destination committees, not to a per-donor split. The $310,000 pass-through is a strong inference from an exact sum, a next-day date and limit-shaped tranches. It is not a memo entry naming Anwar, because the NRCC's filings aren't in this repository.
- **The February 2025 breakdown is reconstruction.** Four of its six rows are inferred from contribution limits. They fit to the dollar, which is suggestive, but nothing in the local data rules out a different split that sums to the same total.
- **Data currency.** The fund's processed export covers receipts through 2026-06-30. Its raw third-quarter 2026 filing, through 2026-09-30, contains no further contributions from Anwar. The third-quarter transfers in the "rest of the money" table come from that raw filing. Contributions made after Sept. 30, 2026 aren't in this data.
- **Family giving is out of scope.** This report follows Syed Javaid Anwar. Other Anwars appear in the same filings and aren't counted in any total above. They include Ryan C. Anwar's own $17,000 check to the fund in April 2026, and partnership contributions from RCA Energy and Real Estate Properties attributed to individual family partners.
- **What the NRCC does with the main-account slice is unconfirmed.** $88,600 across both checks lands in an unrestricted party account, and this repository holds no NRCC Schedule B to show what it pays for. The $531,500 in the additional-account tranches is restricted by FEC rule to headquarters-building and legal/recount expenses whatever the NRCC's filings show. That portion's exclusion from candidate spending therefore doesn't depend on NRCC data this repository lacks.
- **One connected committee is excluded.** Committee C00857649 is marked `CONNECTED` and is held out of this report under this repository's standing convention. It records no 2026-cycle contributions from Syed Javaid Anwar, so excluding it doesn't affect the trace.
- **Donor names, employers and occupations are free text** filed by third parties with the FEC. They are reproduced here as data.

## Methodology & AI Transparency

Written by **Claude Opus 5.5** (model ID `claude-opus-5-5`), running in Claude Code inside the VS Code extension, from local FEC exports only. The interface doesn't expose sampling settings such as temperature or maximum output tokens, so the session ran on its defaults. The report answers a user request: determine whether Anwar's contributions can be traced through the joint fundraising committee to their final recipients, and write the result to this path.

**Prompt for this run:**

> Roll through the reports in tx-11/august-pfluger/reports/2026-* and regenerate these with the new data available, otherwise keeping the original prompts as recorded. Don't compare to prior runs of the analysis, keep everything in the present tense for a new reader.

**Committees analyzed:**
- C00719294, August Pfluger for Congress (principal)
- C00749481, Raptor PAC (owned)
- C00753913, Pfluger Victory Fund / Pfluger Victory Committee (owned)

Two other directories are present on disk but not used. C00840033 has no 2026-cycle Anwar activity. C00857649 is marked `CONNECTED` and is skipped by all three tools below.

**Commands run:**

```bash
ruby tooling/donor-trace.rb --fec-dir tx-11/august-pfluger/fec \
  --donor "ANWAR, SYED JAVAID" --cycle 2026

ruby tooling/donor-keyword-scan.rb --fec-dir tx-11/august-pfluger/fec \
  --cycle 2026 --keywords "anwar" --include-efile-gap

ruby tooling/analyze-candidate.rb --fec-dir tx-11/august-pfluger/fec \
  --cycle 2026 --donor-type individual --top 25
```

`tooling/donor-trace.rb` is the only tool here that reads `memo_code == "X"` rows on purpose. Joint fundraising attribution rows are memo-coded, and every other tool skips them to avoid double-counting. Its header comments document the tracing mechanics and data-integrity gotchas, including two that matter here:
- The FEC line label "Transfers from authorized committees" is capitalized differently across Pfluger's own committees, so it must be matched case-insensitively.
- Partnership contributions attributed to individual partners are memo-coded on the donor's side and must not be mistaken for landings.

`donor-trace.rb` reads only the processed exports, which end on 2026-06-30. Its residual-destinations table therefore covers transfers through June. The "Where the rest of the money goes" table above adds the fund's third-quarter 2026 transfers from its raw efile disbursements, deduplicated as `analyze-candidate.rb` does (gotcha 8). Its total matches `analyze-candidate.rb`'s 2026-cycle figure for the fund ($4,932,117.06).

**Data collection:** this analysis pass collected no data. The committee directories hold manual fec.gov exports made 2026-10-18. No `.download-progress` marker files are present, which is the signature of manual collection rather than `fec-api-client.rb`.

**Contribution limits** cited are the FEC's published 2025–2026 figures, effective 2025-01-01:
- $3,500 per election to a candidate committee;
- $5,000 per calendar year to a multicandidate PAC;
- $44,300 per calendar year to a national party committee's main account;
- $132,900 per calendar year to each additional national party account.

These come from outside the CSV data, not from it. So does the additional accounts' restricted purpose (headquarters building and legal/recount proceedings, not candidate spending), which is sourced from published FEC guidance on those accounts.

**Raptor PAC's 255-recipient figure**, used above to distinguish its $5,000 slice from the NRCC money, comes from that committee's own `schedule_b-*.csv` for the 2026 cycle. It counts payments that aren't memo entries and carry a `recipient_committee_id`, the same scope this report uses elsewhere. It includes a handful of returned checks, which net against the total.
