# TX-11: August Pfluger — Data Center & AI Money in the 2026 Receipts

*Which of August Pfluger's 2026-cycle donors, people and companies alike, trace to the data center and artificial intelligence industry, with a receipt for every dollar.*

## The short version

August Pfluger's four 2026-cycle committees record **$5,767,705.50** in itemized donor receipts. This report traces **$161,550.00 (2.80%)** of it to donors connected to the data center and AI industry.

That figure isn't limited to cloud and hardware companies with an obvious AI-sounding name. It also includes the two industries building AI's physical footprint:
- **telecom and cable carriers**, which lay the fiber that connects data centers: **$62,500**;
- **electric utilities**, which are racing to build the power plants that run them: **$54,500**.

The rest:
- **$23,500:** cloud, hardware and AI-company PACs, including Anthropic's own PAC.
- **$9,050:** four named individuals who work at AI or cloud companies (Anthropic, Google, Nvidia).
- **$5,000:** one AI-defense-tech PAC.
- **$3,500:** one Houston data-center-power executive.
- **$3,500:** two government AI and cloud contractors' PACs.

That is still a small share of Pfluger's overall money: about 2.8 cents of every itemized dollar, against the roughly one-in-five dollars this project's companion oil-and-gas report traces to that industry. Pfluger represents West Texas, and Permian Basin operators dominate his donor list by a wide margin. The data center and AI industry doesn't fund this campaign in any dominant way. But it is present, in specific and traceable amounts, and it is larger and more varied than a narrow "does the company's name sound like AI" scan would find.

## What this says

1. **The headline finding is small but real.** 2.80% of Pfluger's itemized 2026-cycle receipts trace to the data center and AI industry once telecom carriers and power utilities count alongside cloud and hardware names. That's a small fraction next to the share this project's oil-and-gas report traces to Permian Basin money, but it's a traceable presence, not an absence.
2. **The most concrete link to AI isn't a household tech name. It's the power grid.** The $54,500 from electric utilities with specific, current AI-data-center power deals is the best-documented category in this report:
   - Constellation's Three Mile Island restart for Microsoft;
   - Entergy's Louisiana buildout for Meta;
   - AEP's data-center tariff in Ohio.

   It is also the category most easily explained by Pfluger's Energy and Commerce Committee seat rather than any AI-specific interest. FEC data alone can't resolve that tension.
3. **Telecom carriers are the largest category.** The $62,500 from Charter, AT&T, Comcast, Verizon, Cox, Ericsson and T-Mobile appears once the definition widens to include the fiber and wireless backbone AI data centers run on, rather than stopping at companies with an obviously AI-flavored name. Charter Communications is this report's largest identified donor in any category, at $20,000 in six checks.
4. **Big Tech's own money is present, but thin.** Google appears through one executive's $3,500, Nvidia through one engineer's $50, and Oracle, Cisco, Microsoft, Amazon and Meta through small PAC checks. OpenAI, the data-center real-estate firms and the West Texas bitcoin miners are absent entirely.
5. **This report's own authorship is part of the data.** Two Anthropic employees and Anthropic's corporate PAC give a combined $8,000, or 4.95% of this report's kept total, and this report is written with Anthropic's own AI model. That's disclosed below rather than left as an unremarked coincidence.

## The self-referential dollar: Anthropic

This report needs to say plainly what a reader would otherwise have to notice alone. It was researched and drafted by Claude, a model built by Anthropic. The same FEC data it analyzes shows Anthropic money going to Pfluger's committees in the 2026 cycle:
- two Anthropic employees, one of them in "Government Affairs," give a combined $5,500 to his joint fundraising committee;
- Anthropic's corporate PAC gives the same committee $2,500 at the end of September 2026.

Together that's $8,000, or 4.95% of this report's kept total.

This isn't evidence of anything improper. These are ordinary, small, individually disclosed contributions, exactly like every other row in this report. Nothing here suggests the donations relate to Pfluger's committee assignments in any particular way. But a report about AI-industry money in a candidate's fundraising, written by an AI company's own product, has an obvious appearance-of-interest problem if it doesn't name that fact outright. So it's disclosed here instead of left for a reader to discover. No judgment about Pfluger, Anthropic or these donors is made or implied beyond what's stated. The same keyword-match-then-manual-review process was applied to the Anthropic rows as to every other row in this report, and no figure or conclusion was adjusted because of them.

## Caveats

"Data center and AI industry" has no FEC checkbox, and this report defines it more broadly than a narrow "does the company's name sound like AI" test would. Telecom and electric-utility PAC money is one or two steps removed from data centers and AI themselves. A carrier's fiber revenue and a utility's load growth are *inputs* to the AI buildout, not AI products. Counting them is a real interpretive choice, not a neutral fact.

Whether a utility building gas plants for someone else's data center 1,500 miles from TX-11 counts as "data center/AI industry money" in a report about *this candidate* is a judgment call. This report makes it explicitly rather than hiding it, and a reader could reasonably draw the line narrower. Pfluger's Energy and Commerce Committee seat is a genuine, simpler alternative explanation for the telecom and utility rows specifically. FEC data alone can't distinguish "gave because of AI" from "gave because of committee jurisdiction."

As always, legal, fully disclosed campaign contributions are not evidence of anything improper by themselves. A donor whose employer field is blank, or who lists a personal name rather than a company, won't surface here even if their money comes from this industry.
