# TX-11: August Pfluger — Refunded Contributions in the 2026 Cycle

*Every donor August Pfluger's three 2026-cycle committees refunded money to, split between those whose refund lines up with a per-election contribution limit and those refunded for some other reason.*

## The short version

Across August Pfluger's three 2026-cycle committees — **August Pfluger for Congress** (C00719294, principal), **Pfluger Victory Committee** (C00753913, the joint fundraising committee/JFC), and **Raptor PAC** (C00749481, his leadership PAC) — **14 donors were net-refunded a combined $22,317.73** this cycle.

Only **2 of those 14 donors, $15,500.00 (69.4% of every refunded dollar), show the signature of a per-election contribution-limit problem**: a documented aggregate-to-date that reached or exceeded the applicable legal ceiling shortly before the refund. The other **12 donors — $6,817.73, 30.6% of the money** — look nothing like a cap trim. Almost every one of them got back *all or nearly all* of what they gave, in amounts far below any contribution limit — the pattern of a declined/reversed charge, an ineligible source, or a plain donor request, not a campaign paring someone down to a legal maximum.

## Likely hit the per-election contribution limit

**Miles Boldrick Statewide Minerals Co. (Midland, TX) — $12,000.00 refunded by the Pfluger Victory Committee on 2026-05-28.** This is the largest single refund in the dataset. The Victory Committee's own raw filing data shows the source: a single $12,000.00 check from this entity on 2026-04-28, recorded on Schedule A's individual-contribution line (11AI) with FEC entity-type code "ORG" — the coding FEC uses for an LLC or other unincorporated entity that hasn't elected corporate tax treatment, which makes it subject to the *same* $3,500-per-election ceiling as an individual, not a PAC's higher limit. A $12,000 check against a $3,500 ceiling is more than three times over, and the committee refunded the entire amount one month later — consistent with a joint fundraising committee unable to legally allocate an oversized, unattributed check and returning it outright rather than trying to spread the excess across its participants. (Two Boldrick family members — Laurie and Miles individually — are established donors to Pfluger's committees in the 2022 and 2024 cycles, each maxing out at $10,800 and $5,800 respectively across those cycles; "Statewide Minerals Co." itself has no matching contribution row from *any* earlier cycle in this dataset, so it isn't possible to say from local data alone whether this was the same family routing money through a company account or a related-but-distinct contributor.)

**American Petroleum Institute PAC (API PAC), Washington, DC — $3,500.00 refunded by the principal committee (C00719294) on 2026-02-02.** API PAC wrote the Pfluger Victory Committee four checks in the 2026 cycle: $2,500.00 (2025-06-09), $1,000.00 (2025-08-01), $1,500.00 (2026-02-09), and $3,500.00 (2026-03-03) — $8,500.00 total, with the JFC's own filing showing its running aggregate-to-date for this PAC hitting exactly **$5,000.00** — the maximum a *multicandidate* PAC may give a single candidate committee per election — after the final check. The $3,500.00 refund itself, though, was issued by the *principal* committee, and its amount lines up precisely with the first two checks' subtotal ($2,500 + $1,000 = $3,500) that had been earmarked to the principal committee by August 2025. That specific number is also the ceiling for a PAC that has *not yet* qualified as "multicandidate" under FEC rules (the same $3,500 an individual donor is capped at). This dataset can't determine API PAC's multicandidate-registration status on its own — that would require checking FEC's committee database, a lookup this report deliberately did not perform (see Methodology) — but either reading (a multicandidate PAC's running aggregate reaching $5,000, or a not-yet-multicandidate PAC capped at $3,500) is a contribution-limit story, not a processing error.

A few things jump out:

- **Ten of the twelve are retirees, and most got a full or near-full refund of everything they'd given.** Gail, Rondero, Orman, and Penry were refunded to the penny. Piper's, Aven's, and Fening's refunds match (or in Fening's case, essentially match — see Caveats on why it runs $12.50 over) their entire recorded 2026 contribution total. None of these numbers are anywhere close to the $3,500/$5,000 per-election ceilings — the largest refund in this group, Fening's $1,652.40, is under half the individual limit. This is the pattern of a campaign reversing a specific transaction or set of transactions (a declined card, a duplicate charge, a donor's own request to be refunded), not trimming someone down to a legal maximum.
- **Fening and Grimmer are the two most-itemized names in this report** — Fening's $1,639.90 in recorded contributions and $1,652.40 refund span dozens of individual line items as small as $0.95, all dated 2025-09-22; Grimmer's $958.38 and $332.16 span dozens more, as small as $1.00, all dated 2025-06-30. Both patterns read like a payment processor batch-refunding a run of very small, likely recurring or test-sized transactions on a single processing day, not a person-by-person judgment call about any individual gift.
- **Brown County Water District No. 1 is a Texas public utility district — a governmental subdivision, not a person or a private company.** Contributions from certain government-affiliated entities can run into federal source restrictions that have nothing to do with dollar amount; a $250.00 full refund to an entity like this is consistent with the campaign catching and returning an impermissible-source contribution rather than a cap issue, though this report doesn't independently confirm that a legal problem existed.
- **"Gomez" (Chalmette, LA) has no matching 2026 Schedule A contribution row under that name at all**, in any of the three committees. The likely explanation isn't a data-matching failure so much as FEC's own itemization threshold: campaigns aren't required to itemize a donor's *contributions* until that donor's cycle-to-date total exceeds $200, so a series of small enough gifts (this refund breaks down into twelve line items from $0.26 to $100.00, refunded all at once on 2026-06-22) may simply never have crossed that line as receipts, even though the refund itself was itemized on the disbursement side. Whatever the reason, $342.77 is far too small for a limit question to apply.

## Takeaways

1. **Most refund dollars (69.4%) do trace to a contribution-limit problem — but only because of two large checks, not a broad pattern.** Remove Boldrick's $12,000 and API PAC's $3,500 and the remaining 12 refunds average $568 each, nowhere near any legal ceiling.
2. **The single largest refund of the cycle ($12,000, Miles Boldrick Statewide Minerals Co.) is also the one this report can trace with the least certainty** — the underlying contribution only surfaced in raw efile data (see Methodology on what that took to find), and this project's tooling can't independently confirm the entity's relationship to the Boldrick family's own, separately-documented giving history.
3. **Ten of twelve non-cap refunds went to donors whose occupation is filed as "Retired,"** and most of those were full or near-full refunds of small-dollar gifts — a pattern that looks like transaction-level reversals (declined charges, processor corrections, donor requests) rather than judgment calls about any individual person.
4. **A government entity (Brown County Water District No. 1) shows up among the refunded donors** — worth a human's attention as a possible impermissible-source correction, independent of any contribution-limit question.
5. **This report only exists because of two real gaps in this project's existing tooling**, both fixed as part of producing it (see Methodology): an efile amendment-detection routine that was silently collapsing genuinely separate same-day, same-amount transactions into one, and a cap-check that couldn't see LLC/entity-coded contributions filed under the individual contribution line. Both fixes are now permanent parts of `analyze-candidate.rb`, not one-off patches to this report.

## Caveats

**Donor identity is matched between Schedule B (refunds) and Schedule A (contributions) by name only**, because Schedule B refund rows carry no employer field to disambiguate two people who share a name — a real, if narrow, risk of misattributing employer/occupation/city/state or a cap-check result to the wrong same-named person. None of the 14 donors here have an obvious same-name collision risk (no two share a name), but this isn't independently verified beyond that.

**The $3,500 individual / $5,000 multicandidate-PAC per-election limits used throughout this report are the published 2025–2026 cycle limits, not something derived from the data itself** — hard-coded into the underlying tool and worth re-confirming against FEC.gov if a future cycle's limits change.

**Money moving through Pfluger's JFC (Pfluger Victory Committee) genuinely complicates "did this donor hit the limit."** A donor's real limit is per candidate, across *every* authorized committee combined — but a JFC also pools money for participants other than Pfluger's own committees (this cycle: the NRCC and others), and this report's cap check only has visibility into what Pfluger's own three collected committees show. The API PAC case above is the clearest example: its refund's exact dollar amount only makes sense once you separate "the JFC's own combined receipts from this donor" from "how much of that had actually been earmarked to the principal committee specifically" — two different numbers this report had to reconcile by hand.

**Fening, Daniel's refund ($1,652.40) is $12.50 more than his recorded 2026 contribution total ($1,639.90).** This report doesn't resolve the gap; it's small enough to plausibly be a processing fee reversal, a contribution recorded under a slightly different scope than this report's CAP_CHECK_LABELS allowlist captures, or a minor data quirk, but it's flagged here rather than silently rounded away.

**No claim is made that any refund here reflects an actual FEC violation.** A refund is exactly the mechanism campaigns are supposed to use to stay compliant — every refund in this report represents the system working, not evidence of wrongdoing. This report traces the money and offers a data-grounded read of *why* a refund likely happened; it does not, and cannot, establish legal fault.

This is also a small dataset — 14 donors, $22,317.73 — so every percentage above is sensitive to a single check; the Boldrick refund alone is more than half the "at cap" total and 53.8% of every dollar refunded this cycle.

**Tooling — new `--refunds` flag added to `tooling/analyze-candidate.rb`:** No existing flag covered this question, and this project's `README.md` explicitly asks that new analysis needs be added to the shared tool rather than written as a parallel one-off script, so this report required extending `analyze-candidate.rb` itself with a new `analyze_refunds` method and `--refunds` CLI flag (see the tool's own header comments for the full design rationale) before this report could be written. In summary: it identifies Schedule B's refund line (`Refunds of Contributions...` on processed exports; numeric lines 20A/20B/20C/28A/28B/28C on raw efile), builds a donor contribution profile from Schedule A (including money earmarked through the JFC's "Transfers from authorized committees" line, which counts against the same per-election limit as a direct check even though it's excluded from this project's normal "Key Donors" totals), and classifies each refunded donor by comparing FEC's own `contributor_aggregate_ytd` field against the published 2025–2026 per-election limits ($3,500 individual, $5,000 multicandidate PAC). Command run:
```
ruby tooling/analyze-candidate.rb --fec-dir tx-11/august-pfluger/fec --cycle 2026 --refunds --format json --out /tmp/pfluger-refunds.json
```

**Two tool-level data-integrity fixes made while building this report** (both now permanent, not specific to this report):

1. **Efile amendment-dedup was silently collapsing genuine repeat transactions.** The existing gap-fill logic (added for a prior report, documented as "gotcha 8") collapses efile rows sharing the same date/amount/counterparty, on the theory that this signature identifies an original-plus-corrected-amendment pair. Testing this report's own refund data against that logic surfaced a case (donor "Gomez," 12 refund line items on 2026-06-22, five of them separately $35.00) where the *same* logic collapsed five genuinely distinct transactions down to one, undercounting that donor's true refund total by $140.26. Cross-checking against the documented amendment case that justified the original logic (Claire Reynolds's committee, transaction pair SA11AI.5956/SA11C.6056) showed the real amendment pair spans two different `file_number`s, while Gomez's 12 rows all share one `file_number` — so the fix narrows the collapse to only trigger across multiple file numbers, preserving the original amendment-detection behavior while no longer over-collapsing same-submission repeats. Verified against both cases after the fix (Reynolds's pair still collapses to $5,000, not $10,000; Gomez's total rose from $202.51 to the correct $342.77).
2. **The cap-check couldn't see entity-coded (ORG) contributions on the individual line, or classify efile-only donors by election at all.** Raw efile Schedule A codes some LLC/unincorporated-entity contributors as `entity_type` "ORG" rather than "IND," even when filed on the individual contribution line (11AI) — this project's existing `EFILE_INDIVIDUAL_LINES` check only recognized "IND," so the Boldrick $12,000 contribution was invisible to this report's cap check entirely until fixed. Separately, raw efile Schedule A has no `election_type` column at all (that's processed-export-only) — its closest equivalent, `pgo`, is blank on some rows, including Boldrick's — so a naive port of the existing per-election bucketing logic would have silently dropped that row's aggregate rather than flagging it. Both gaps are fixed in `analyze_refunds` specifically (not the broader `analyze_donors`/`analyze_donor_geography` methods, which weren't touched, to avoid an unreviewed change to this project's main donor totals).

**Verification performed:** every dollar figure and donor detail above was checked directly against the source CSVs before publication — including manually reconstructing the API PAC contribution/refund timeline row-by-row across all three committees (confirming the $8,500 total isn't inflated by the JFC-earmark memo rows that mirror the same money onto the principal committee's own Schedule A, per this tool's existing memo-code-X exclusion rule), and manually searching raw efile data for a "Boldrick"/"Minerals" contribution row after the tool's own name-join initially came up empty, which is what surfaced both fixes described above.
