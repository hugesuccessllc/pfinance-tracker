# TX-11: August Pfluger — Refunded Contributions in the 2026 Cycle

*Every donor August Pfluger's 2026-cycle committees refunded money to, split between those whose refund lines up with a per-election contribution limit and those refunded for some other reason.*

## The short version

Across August Pfluger's four 2026-cycle committees — **August Pfluger for Congress** (C00719294, principal), **Pfluger Victory Fund** (C00753913, joint fundraising committee/JFC), **Raptor PAC** (C00749481, leadership PAC), and **PFriends of Pfluger** (C00840033, a second, much smaller JFC) — **15 donors were net-refunded a combined $27,317.73** this cycle.

**3 of those 15 donors, $20,500.00 (75.0% of every refunded dollar), show the signature of a per-election contribution-limit problem**: a documented aggregate-to-date that reached or exceeded the applicable legal ceiling shortly before the refund. The other **12 donors — $6,817.73, 25.0% of the money** — look nothing like a cap trim. Almost every one of them got back *all or nearly all* of what they gave, in amounts far below any contribution limit — the pattern of a declined/reversed charge, an ineligible source, or a plain donor request, not a campaign paring someone down to a legal maximum.

## Likely hit the per-election contribution limit

**Miles Boldrick Statewide Minerals Co. (Midland, TX) — $12,000.00 refunded by the Pfluger Victory Fund on 2026-05-28.** This is the largest single refund in the dataset. A single $12,000.00 check from this entity landed on 2026-04-28, filed on Schedule A's individual-contribution line (11AI) with FEC entity-type code "ORG" — the coding FEC uses for an LLC or other unincorporated entity that hasn't elected corporate tax treatment, which makes it subject to the *same* $3,500-per-election ceiling as an individual, not a PAC's higher limit. A $12,000 check against a $3,500 ceiling is more than three times over, and the committee refunded the entire amount one month later — consistent with a joint fundraising committee unable to legally allocate an oversized, unattributed check and returning it outright rather than trying to spread the excess across its participants. (Two Boldrick family members — Laurie and Miles individually — are established donors to Pfluger's committees in the 2022 and 2024 cycles, each maxing out at $10,800 and $5,800 respectively across those cycles; "Statewide Minerals Co." itself has no matching contribution row from *any* earlier cycle in this dataset, so it isn't possible to say from local data alone whether this was the same family routing money through a company account or a related-but-distinct contributor.)

**E-PAC (Glens Falls, NY) — $5,000.00 refunded by PFriends of Pfluger (C00840033) on 2026-06-18.** This is the cleanest cap case in the dataset. E-PAC — the leadership PAC founded by Rep. Elise Stefanik (R-NY), also known as Elevate PAC — wrote a single $5,000.00 check to PFriends of Pfluger, and its aggregate-to-date for that committee hit exactly $5,000.00 — the maximum a multicandidate PAC may give a single candidate committee per election — with that one check. The full $5,000.00 came back. Notably, this $5,000.00 in and $5,000.00 out is essentially the entirety of PFriends of Pfluger's 2026-cycle activity; the committee otherwise shows no other 2026 transactions in this dataset.

**American Petroleum Institute PAC (API PAC), Washington, DC — $3,500.00 refunded by the principal committee (C00719294) on 2026-02-02.** API PAC wrote the Pfluger Victory Fund four checks in the 2026 cycle: $2,500.00 (2025-06-09), $1,000.00 (2025-08-01), $1,500.00 (2026-02-09), and $3,500.00 (2026-03-03) — $8,500.00 total, with the JFC's own filing showing its running aggregate-to-date for this PAC hitting exactly **$5,000.00** — the maximum a *multicandidate* PAC may give a single candidate committee per election — after the final check. The $3,500.00 refund itself, though, was issued by the *principal* committee, and its amount lines up precisely with the first two checks' subtotal ($2,500 + $1,000 = $3,500) that had been earmarked to the principal committee by August 2025. That specific number is also the ceiling for a PAC that has *not yet* qualified as "multicandidate" under FEC rules (the same $3,500 an individual donor is capped at). This dataset can't determine API PAC's multicandidate-registration status on its own — that would require checking FEC's committee database, a lookup this report deliberately did not perform (see Methodology) — but either reading (a multicandidate PAC's running aggregate reaching $5,000, or a not-yet-multicandidate PAC capped at $3,500) is a contribution-limit story, not a processing error.

A few things jump out:

- **Ten of the twelve are retirees, and most got a full or near-full refund of everything they'd given.** Piper, Gail, Aven, Rondero, Orman, and Penry were refunded to the penny. None of these numbers are anywhere close to the $3,500/$5,000 per-election ceilings — the largest refund in this group, Fening's $1,652.40, is under half the individual limit. This is the pattern of a campaign reversing a specific transaction or set of transactions (a declined card, a duplicate charge, a donor's own request to be refunded), not trimming someone down to a legal maximum.
- **Fening and Grimmer are the two most-itemized names in this report** — Fening's $1,639.90 in recorded contributions and $1,652.40 refund span dozens of individual line items as small as $0.95, all dated 2025-09-22; Grimmer's $958.38 and $332.16 span dozens more, as small as $1.00, all dated 2025-06-30. Both patterns read like a payment processor batch-refunding a run of very small, likely recurring or test-sized transactions on a single processing day, not a person-by-person judgment call about any individual gift.
- **Brown County Water District No. 1 is a Texas public utility district — a governmental subdivision, not a person or a private company.** Contributions from certain government-affiliated entities can run into federal source restrictions that have nothing to do with dollar amount; a $250.00 full refund to an entity like this is consistent with the campaign catching and returning an impermissible-source contribution rather than a cap issue, though this report doesn't independently confirm that a legal problem existed.
- **Gomez, Kathleen's refund ($342.77) is $14.51 more than her recorded contribution total ($328.26).** Her ten contribution rows (2026-05-19 through 2026-06-01, $0.26 to $100.00) match ten of her twelve refund line items exactly; the other two refund rows — a second $0.26 and a $14.25 — have no matching contribution row at all. That's small enough to be a processing artifact rather than a sign of a real data problem, but it's flagged here rather than silently netted away.

## Takeaways

1. **Most refund dollars (75.0%) do trace to a contribution-limit problem — but only because of three large checks, not a broad pattern.** Remove Boldrick's $12,000, E-PAC's $5,000, and API PAC's $3,500, and the remaining 12 refunds average $568.14 each, nowhere near any legal ceiling.
2. **The cleanest cap case in the dataset belongs to Pfluger's smallest, least-active committee.** E-PAC's $5,000 check to PFriends of Pfluger hit the multicandidate-PAC limit exactly and came back in full — and that single in-and-out pair is essentially the entire 2026-cycle footprint of that committee.
3. **The single largest refund of the cycle ($12,000, Miles Boldrick Statewide Minerals Co.) is also the one this report can trace with the least certainty about the underlying relationship** — its entity coding (an LLC-style contributor filed on the individual line) explains why it hit the individual limit rather than a PAC's higher one, but this project's tooling can't independently confirm the entity's relationship to the Boldrick family's own, separately-documented giving history.
4. **Ten of twelve non-cap refunds went to donors whose occupation is filed as "Retired,"** and most of those were full or near-full refunds of small-dollar gifts — a pattern that looks like transaction-level reversals (declined charges, processor corrections, donor requests) rather than judgment calls about any individual person.
5. **A government entity (Brown County Water District No. 1) shows up among the refunded donors** — worth a human's attention as a possible impermissible-source correction, independent of any contribution-limit question.

## Caveats

**Donor identity is matched between Schedule B (refunds) and Schedule A (contributions) by name only**, because Schedule B refund rows carry no employer field to disambiguate two people who share a name — a real, if narrow, risk of misattributing employer/occupation/city/state or a cap-check result to the wrong same-named person. None of the 15 donors here have an obvious same-name collision risk (no two share an identical name), but this isn't independently verified beyond that.

**The $3,500 individual / $5,000 multicandidate-PAC per-election limits used throughout this report are the published 2025–2026 cycle limits, not something derived from the data itself** — hard-coded into the underlying tool and worth re-confirming against FEC.gov if a future cycle's limits change.

**Money moving through Pfluger's JFCs genuinely complicates "did this donor hit the limit."** A donor's real limit is per candidate, across *every* authorized committee combined — but a JFC also pools money for participants other than Pfluger's own committees (this cycle: the NRCC and others), and this report's cap check only has visibility into what Pfluger's own four collected committees show. The API PAC case above is the clearest example: its refund's exact dollar amount only makes sense once you separate "the JFC's own combined receipts from this donor" from "how much of that had actually been earmarked to the principal committee specifically" — two different numbers this report had to reconcile by hand.

**Fening, Daniel's refund ($1,652.40) is $12.50 more than his recorded 2026 contribution total ($1,639.90), and Gomez, Kathleen's ($342.77 vs. $328.26) is off by $14.51 in the same direction** — see "Refunded for other reasons" above for the Gomez breakdown. Neither gap is resolved here; both are small enough to plausibly be a processing-fee reversal, a contribution recorded outside this report's CAP_CHECK_LABELS scope, or a minor data quirk, but they're flagged rather than silently rounded away.

**No claim is made that any refund here reflects an actual FEC violation.** A refund is exactly the mechanism campaigns are supposed to use to stay compliant — every refund in this report represents the system working, not evidence of wrongdoing. This report traces the money and offers a data-grounded read of *why* a refund likely happened; it does not, and cannot, establish legal fault.

This is also a small dataset — 15 donors, $27,317.73 — so every percentage above is sensitive to a single check; the Boldrick refund alone is 43.9% of every dollar refunded this cycle, and all three at-cap refunds together are 75.0%.

**A tool-level data-integrity fix made while producing this report** (now permanent, not specific to this report, and covered by a new regression test in `tooling/spec/analyze_candidate/fec_analyzer_spec.rb`): the Miles Boldrick contribution that a prior version of this report traced through raw efile data now sits in the *processed* `schedule_a` export instead (the export itself has been refreshed since). That processed row turned out to be invisible to the cap check for two reasons the efile-only code path had already solved but the processed-export path hadn't: (1) FEC's own `is_individual` column reads "f" for this row (entity_type "ORG"), which would have applied the higher $5,000 multicandidate-PAC limit instead of the $3,500 individual one; and (2) the row's `election_type` field is blank, and the cap-check logic silently dropped any contribution with no election_type rather than counting it under an "unknown election" bucket. Both are now fixed directly in `analyze_refunds`: which of the two `DONOR_LABELS` a processed row is filed under (individual-contributor line vs. political-committee line) now determines the limit used, the same way the efile path already used `entity_type`/line-number logic; and a blank `election_type`, on either the processed or efile path, now falls into an explicit `"UNKNOWN (no election_type on source row)"` bucket rather than vanishing. Verified against the full test suite (194 examples, 0 failures) plus a new dedicated case for this exact scenario.
