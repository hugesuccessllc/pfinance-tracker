# TX-11: August Pfluger — Oil & Gas Money, 2020–2026

*Which of August Pfluger's donors, people and companies alike, trace to the oil and gas industry, across every cycle he has run, with a receipt for every dollar.*

## The short version

Since August Pfluger's first campaign filing in 2019, his four committees have taken in **$17,659,740.61** in itemized donor receipts. Those committees are his campaign, his leadership PAC (Raptor PAC), and two joint fundraising committees. Of that total, **$3,653,366.31 (20.7%, about one dollar in every five)** comes from donors this report can independently tie to the oil and gas industry:
- company executives;
- landmen (the industry's lease-and-title specialists) and mineral-rights owners;
- oilfield-service operators;
- the PACs of drillers, refiners and pipeline companies.

The current 2026 cycle produces both the largest dollar total and the largest share of any cycle in Pfluger's career. Oil-and-gas money is **23.5%** of the cycle's receipts ($1,357,260.71 of $5,767,705.50), more than double the 2020-cycle dollar figure. That's no surprise for a member who represents West Texas and sits on the House Energy and Commerce Committee; the Permian Basin is the foundation of his fundraising. What this report adds is the receipt-level detail: which companies, which executives, how much, and where to check it yourself.

## One committee carries most of it

Of the $3,653,366.31 counted here:
- **$2,674,330.00 (73.2%)** flows through the **Pfluger Victory Committee**, the current joint fundraising committee;
- **$857,986.31 (23.5%)** goes directly to the principal campaign committee;
- **$83,550.00 (2.3%)** goes to **Raptor PAC**;
- **$37,500.00 (1.0%)** goes to the smaller, earlier **Pfriends of Pfluger** JFC.

Within the Pfluger Victory Committee alone, oil-and-gas money is **24.5%** of its entire $10,917,490.79 in career itemized receipts.

That lopsidedness matches a pattern this project documents for Pfluger generally. A joint fundraising committee can legally accept much larger checks than a single campaign committee, because its per-donor limit is the sum of all the participating committees' own limits. That is the mechanism that makes a $332,100 single-donor check possible at all. Anyone who pulls only the principal committee's report sees less than a quarter of this money.

## What counted, and what didn't

This report starts from an automated keyword scan (`tooling/donor-keyword-scan.rb`). It searches every donor's name, employer and occupation across all four committees and every cycle, including the raw third-quarter 2026 filings. Every one of the 1,096 raw matches was then reviewed: reading the row, and where the connection wasn't obvious, checking what the named company does. The review keeps 947 rows ($3,653,366.31) and excludes 149 rows ($160,105.06), in five categories:

- **Surname coincidences, not industry connections ($20,955.06 excluded).** "Gas" and "oil" are substrings of ordinary surnames. Every donor below matches on nothing but their last name:
  - Roland **Dugas**
  - Robert **Gaston**
  - Luda **Gashenko** (51 small recurring checks)
  - Ken **Gaspar**
  - Ted **Legasey**
  - Burt **Forgason**
  - Jason and Charlotte **Pendergast**, whose actual employers are an aggregates company and a tax-prep firm

  One near-namesake, **Clay Gaspar**, is *kept*: his employer is WPX Energy, a real Permian/Bakken producer later merged into Devon Energy.
- **A different kind of "exploration," and a few unrelated "resources" ($47,300 excluded).**
  - The **SpaceX** PAC (Space Exploration Technologies Corp.) matches on "exploration" and has nothing to do with oil and gas.
  - Douglass Robison's **Natura Resources** is an advanced-nuclear company.
  - **Maas Energy Works** is a California renewable-natural-gas company that turns dairy manure into biogas.
  - **OCIS Intelligent Energy** builds natural-gas-and-battery power infrastructure for data centers. That is closer to the AI power-buildout story in this project's `2026-datacenters.md` report than to drilling.
  - **Cirrus Resources** (Randal J. Gardner) stays excluded pending confirmation of what the company does.
- **Electric-utility and nuclear PACs that match only on "energy," "operating," or "resources" ($60,500 excluded).** These are regulated electric utilities or nuclear-technology firms:
  - Constellation Energy, Sempra Energy, NextEra Energy, Xcel Energy, PG&E, DTE Energy, CMS Energy
  - NRG Energy and Vistra, the latter including its CEO
  - Holtec International, Southern Nuclear Operating Company, and the Nuclear Energy Institute

  Several are the same companies `2026-datacenters.md` documents as riding the AI-driven power-demand boom. That's a different industry from Permian-style oil and gas, even where the word "energy" is shared.
- **Medical practices, a bank, and an HR title named after the region, not the industry ($15,750 excluded).** "Permian Basin Pain Management," "Basin Emergency Physicians," "Permian Internal Medicine," "Permian Gastroenterology Associates" and "Permian Womans Center" are medical practices serving Midland and Odessa. Their physicians' employer fields happen to contain "permian," or "gas" as in gastroenterology. A Frost Bank regional president whose title mentions the Permian region, and a Comanche County Electric Cooperative "Director of Human Resources," complete this category.
- **Other regional names and unrelated businesses ($15,600 excluded).** These match on "basin," "permian," "wildcat," "energy" or "gas" but aren't oil and gas companies:
  - Basin Computer Services
  - Permian Mediation PLLC, a law practice
  - Permian Wealth Management
  - Permian Basin Kidney Care
  - Basin Hill Group, whose line of business is unconfirmed
  - Wildcat Ranch
  - Airgas, an industrial-gas supplier
  - Tri Global Energy, a wind and solar developer
  - LF Bioenergy, a biofuels company
  - Berkshire Hathaway Energy's PAC, an electric utility holding company

The scope also follows the rules this project uses to avoid double-counting a candidate's own money. Only receipt rows the FEC's `line_number_label` field marks as **"Contributions From Individuals"** or **"Contributions From Other Political Committees"** count as a donor giving money. A "Transfers from authorized committees" row never counts, because that's how a joint fundraising committee's pooled proceeds move to a participating committee.

## What this says

1. **One family accounts for more than a quarter of everything this report counts.** The Anwars' $1,012,100 since 2019 is more than any other single family or company cluster has given Pfluger's committees, by a factor of about four.
2. **It's concentrated, not diffuse, and it's at a career high.** A compact circle of Permian Basin operators, many related by family, employer or business partnership, accounts for most of the money. The total has grown from $578,407.12 in Pfluger's first race to $1,357,260.71 in the current cycle.
3. **The joint fundraising committee is the mechanism, not a coincidence.** Nearly three-quarters of all oil-and-gas money goes through the joint fundraising vehicles, which can legally accept far larger checks than the campaign itself. That's the same structural point this project makes about Pfluger's fundraising generally, isolated here to a single industry across his whole career.
4. **The geography tracks the district.** Midland and Odessa, the heart of the Permian Basin and part of TX-11, dominate the donor list in every cycle, which is what you'd expect from a member representing that district.
5. **None of this is unusual or hidden.** Every donor and dollar here is exactly what the committees report to the FEC. The finding isn't that the money exists. It's how much of it there is, and how central it is, once someone adds it up.

## Caveats

This is a keyword-and-manual-review exercise, not an industry classification handed down by the FEC; there is no "oil and gas" checkbox on a Schedule A filing. A donor's occupation and employer fields are free text supplied by the filer, and they can be stale, misspelled or blank. A company spelled differently, or missing from the keyword list, would not appear here even if it belongs.

Industry classification is an editorial judgment made row by row in this report. Is a gas-utility PAC "oil and gas industry" money? Is a nuclear-adjacent "energy" PAC? Reasonable people could draw the line differently, particularly for the gas-utility cluster flagged above.

Every dollar figure above was checked against the source CSV at the cited file and line; readers are encouraged to open those files and check the work themselves. As always, legal, fully disclosed campaign contributions are not evidence of anything improper by themselves. This report counts and traces the money and leaves judgment about what it means to the reader.
