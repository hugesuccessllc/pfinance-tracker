# TX-11: August Pfluger — Oil & Gas Money, 2020–2026

*A look at exactly which of August Pfluger's donors — people and companies — trace to the oil and gas industry, across every cycle he's run, with a receipt for every dollar.*

## The short version

Since August Pfluger's first campaign filing in 2019, his four campaign committees have taken in **$17,209,793.54** in itemized donor receipts. Of that, **$3,647,224.81 — 21.2%, better than one dollar in every five** — comes from donors this report can independently tie to the oil and gas industry: company executives, landmen, mineral-rights owners, oilfield-service operators, and the PACs of drillers, refiners, and pipeline companies.

That share hasn't been flat. In the current 2026 cycle, oil-and-gas money is **25.2%** of the cycle's receipts ($1,338,669.21 of $5,317,758.43) — both the largest dollar total and the largest share of any cycle in Pfluger's career, and more than double the 2020-cycle dollar figure. This isn't a surprise for a candidate who represents West Texas and sits on the Energy and Commerce Committee — Pfluger's disclosures already signal that the Permian Basin is the foundation of his fundraising. What this report adds is the receipt-level detail: which companies, which executives, how much, and where to verify it yourself.

## One family, more than a quarter of every dollar

**Syed Javaid Anwar** — president and CEO of Midland-based **Midland Energy, Inc.** — has given **$979,300** to Pfluger's committees across ten checks since 2019, headlined by **$332,100** on 2025-02-07 and **$310,000** on 2026-03-16, both to the Pfluger Victory Committee (`fec/C00753913/schedule_a-2026-08-15T11_47_21.csv:3304`, `:3303`). His son, **Ryan Anwar** (listed variously as "Founder" and "Owner" at Midland Energy), has given another **$32,800** across five checks of his own. Combined, the Anwar family accounts for **$1,012,100** — nearly **28% of every oil-and-gas dollar** counted in this report, from one family. Anwar has previously been reported as a major backer of Governor Greg Abbott and a Texas Higher Education Coordinating Board appointee ([E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/major-texas-oil-donor-emerges-this-cycle-who-is-he/); [Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/oil-titans-hildebrand-anwar-break-for-trump-in-cash-race)).

## One committee carries most of it

Of the $3,647,224.81 kept total, **$2,666,330.00 (73.1%)** flowed through the **Pfluger Victory Committee/PFriends of Pfluger family of joint fundraising committees**, versus **$859,844.81 (23.6%)** given directly to the principal campaign committee, **$83,550.00 (2.3%)** to **Raptor PAC**, and **$37,500.00 (1.0%)** to the smaller, earlier PFriends of Pfluger JFC. Within the current JFC alone, oil-and-gas money is **25.2%** of its entire $10,575,590.79 in career itemized receipts.

That lopsidedness matches a pattern this project has already documented for Pfluger: a JFC can legally accept much larger checks than a single campaign committee (the per-donor limit is the sum of all the participating committees' own limits), which is exactly the mechanism that let a $332,100 single-donor check happen at all. Anyone who only pulled the principal committee's report would see less than a quarter of this.

By cycle, the money has grown almost every time Pfluger has run:

| Cycle | Oil & gas total | Share of that cycle's receipts |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2020 (first race) | $585,857.12 | 23.1% |
| 2022 | $909,220.00 | 21.3% |

2024 is the one dip in an otherwise steady climb — not because the industry pulled back, but because 2026 is where the Anwar family's largest single checks landed, and because Pfluger's overall fundraising base (donor count, small-dollar volume) grew faster than the Permian-specific total that cycle. The 2026 figure is both the largest dollar total and the largest share of any cycle in his career so far.

## What counted, and what didn't

This report started from an automated keyword scan (`tooling/donor-keyword-scan.rb`) against every donor's name, employer, and occupation across all four committees and every cycle, then every one of the 1,071 raw matches was checked — reading the row, and where the connection wasn't obvious, checking the company's own name to confirm what it actually does. That review kept 946 rows ($3,647,224.81) and excluded 125 rows ($123,655.06), in four categories:

- **Surname coincidences, not industry connections ($19,205.06 excluded).** "Gas" and "oil" are substrings of ordinary surnames — Roland **Dugas**, Robert **Gaston**, Luda **Gashenko** (51 small recurring checks), Ken **Gaspar**, Burt **Forgason**, and both Jason and Charlotte **Pendergast** (whose actual employers are an aggregates company and a tax-prep firm) all matched on nothing but their last names, across every cycle they appear in. One near-namesake, **Clay Gaspar**, was *kept*: his employer is WPX Energy, a real Permian/Bakken producer later merged into Devon Energy — a genuine match despite the same surname pattern.
- **A different kind of "exploration" and a few unrelated "resources" ($47,300 excluded).** The Space Exploration Technologies Corp. PAC — SpaceX — matched on "exploration" and has nothing to do with oil and gas. Douglass Robison's **Natura Resources** is an advanced-nuclear company. **Maas Energy Works** turns out to be a California renewable-natural-gas company that converts dairy manure into biogas. **OCIS Intelligent Energy** builds natural-gas-and-battery power infrastructure for data centers, closer to the AI power-buildout story documented in this project's `2026-datacenters.md` report than to a drilling company. **Cirrus Resources** (Randal J. Gardner) remains excluded pending confirmation of its line of business — Texas corporate records confirm the company and the donor are the same entity, but not what it actually does.
- **Electric-utility and nuclear PACs matching only on "energy," "operating," or "resources" ($48,000 excluded).** Constellation Energy, Sempra Energy, NextEra Energy, Xcel Energy, Holtec International, PG&E, Southern Nuclear Operating Company, Vistra, NRG Energy, DTE Energy, CMS Energy, and the Nuclear Energy Institute are all regulated electric utilities or nuclear-technology firms — several of them the same companies this project's `2026-datacenters.md` report documents as riding the AI-driven power-demand boom, which is a different industry from Permian-style oil and gas even where the word "energy" is shared.
- **Medical practices, a bank, and an HR title named after the region, not the industry ($9,150 excluded).** "Permian Basin Pain Management," "Basin Emergency Physicians," "Permian Internal Medicine," "Permian Gastroenterology Associates," and "Permian Womans Center" are all medical practices serving Midland/Odessa, not oil companies — physicians whose employer field happened to match "permian" or "gas" (as in gastroenterology). A Frost Bank regional president whose title referenced the Permian region, and a Comanche County Electric Cooperative employee whose occupation is literally "Director of Human Resources," round out this category.

The scoping also follows the rules this project already uses to avoid double-counting a candidate's own money: only receipt rows the FEC's own `line_number_label` field marks as **"Contributions From Individuals"** or **"Contributions From Other Political Committees"** count as a donor giving money — never a "Transfers from authorized committees" row, which is how a JFC's own pooled proceeds move to a participating committee.

## What this actually says

1. **One family alone accounts for more than a quarter of everything this report counts.** The Anwars' $1,012,100 since 2019 is a bigger single-family total than most other donors' entire careers combined.
2. **It's concentrated, not diffuse, and it's grown every cycle except one.** A compact circle of Permian Basin operators — many related by family, employer, or business partnership — account for most of the money, and the total has climbed from $585,857.12 in Pfluger's first race to $1,338,669.21 in the current cycle.
3. **The JFC is the mechanism, not a coincidence.** Nearly three-quarters of all oil-and-gas money has gone through the joint fundraising vehicles that can legally accept far larger checks than the campaign itself — the same structural point this project has made about Pfluger's fundraising generally, just now isolated to a single industry across his whole career.
4. **Geography tracks the district.** Midland and Odessa — the heart of the Permian Basin and part of TX-11 — dominate the donor list across every cycle, which is exactly what you'd expect from a member representing that district.
5. **None of this is unusual or hidden.** Every donor and dollar here is exactly what the candidate reported to the FEC. The finding isn't that the money exists — it's how much of it there is, and how consistently it's grown, once someone actually adds it up.

## Caveats

This is a keyword-and-manual-review exercise, not an industry classification handed down by the FEC — there is no "oil and gas" checkbox on a Schedule A filing. A donor's occupation or employer field is free text supplied by the filer, and it can be stale, misspelled, or simply absent. A differently-worded or unlisted company name would not appear here even if it belongs. Industry classification — is a gas-utility PAC "oil and gas industry" money, is a nuclear-adjacent "energy" PAC — is an editorial judgment made row-by-row in this report, not an FEC-assigned category; reasonable people could draw the line differently, particularly for the gas-utility cluster flagged explicitly above. Every dollar figure above was checked against the source CSV at the cited file and line before publication; readers are encouraged to open those files and check the work themselves. As always: legal, fully disclosed campaign contributions are not evidence of anything improper by themselves — this report counts and traces the money, and leaves judgment about what it means to the reader.
