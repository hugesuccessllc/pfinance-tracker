# TX-11: August Pfluger — Out-of-State Money in the 2026 Receipts

*What share of Pfluger's 2026-cycle donor receipts — by dollar value AND by raw contribution count — comes from outside Texas, across all three of his committees, and who's behind it.*

## The short version

Of the **$5,239,920.63** in itemized donor receipts recorded across August Pfluger's three 2026-cycle committees (10,148 contributions total), **$1,737,648.72 — 33.2% of the money — came from outside Texas**, but those out-of-state donors accounted for **8,546 of the 10,148 contributions — 84.2% of every single itemized transaction filed**. (A residual $13,850.00 across 8 contributions has a blank contributor-state field and is excluded from both percentages; see Caveats.)

That gap — a third of the dollars, five-sixths of the paperwork — isn't a rounding artifact. It's two structurally different donor pools sitting inside the same disclosures: a small number of very large Texas checks, mostly oil-and-gas money already documented in this project's other Pfluger reports, and a much larger, geographically scattered base of small, repeat, often sub-$10 contributions from retirees around the country. Both are real, both are legal, and neither is visible if you look only at a top-donor list or only at a dollar total.

## Two different money machines, one committee network

Pfluger's money runs through three committees — **August Pfluger for Congress** (his principal committee, C00719294), **Raptor PAC** (his leadership PAC, C00749481), and the **Pfluger Victory Committee** (a joint fundraising committee, or JFC, C00753913) — and the Texas/out-of-state split looks completely different in each one:

| Committee | Total | Texas ($ / n) | Out-of-state ($ / n) |
|---|---|---|---|
| August Pfluger for Congress | $638,070.63 (9,032 contributions) | $194,371.91 (30.5%) / 1,013 (11.2%) | $440,198.72 (69.0%) / **8,016 (88.7%)** |
| Raptor PAC | $52,000.00 (14 contributions) | $5,000.00 (9.6%) / 1 (7.1%) | $47,000.00 (90.4%) / 13 (92.9%) |
| Pfluger Victory Committee (JFC) | $4,549,850.00 (1,102 contributions) | $3,289,050.00 (72.3%) / 580 (52.6%) | $1,250,450.00 (27.5%) / 517 (46.9%) |

Three takeaways sit right in that table:

1. **Nearly all of the out-of-state contribution *count* (8,016 of 8,546, 93.8%) runs through the principal campaign committee**, not the JFC. That's the small-dollar, high-frequency donor base described below.
2. **The JFC — the vehicle for the big checks — is Pfluger's most Texas-weighted committee by dollars (72.3%)**, consistent with what this project's other Pfluger reports have already found: Permian Basin oil money, concentrated in a handful of huge checks (see `README.md`'s Key Donors section and `reports/2026-oil-and-gas.md`).
3. **Raptor PAC, the leadership PAC, is almost entirely out-of-state money** — but it's tiny (14 total contributions), so its 90.4% doesn't move the overall picture much; it's dominated by national trade-association and corporate PAC checks, the same kind of donor as the JFC's out-of-state PAC money below.

## The other 8,000 contributions: a nationwide base of small, repeat, retiree donors

The dollar figures above only explain a fraction of the **8,546 out-of-state contributions**. The volume comes from somewhere else entirely, and it's a genuinely striking pattern once isolated: of the 6,824 out-of-state contributions attributed to individual donors (as opposed to PACs), **6,011 — 88.1% — list "RETIRED" as the donor's occupation**, and together they total just **$145,409.14**. Put another way: well over half of *every single itemized contribution* Pfluger's three committees recorded this cycle — 6,011 of 10,148, from Texas and everywhere else combined — is a small donation from someone who told the FEC they're retired.

These aren't one-time gifts. The same donors give over and over, in small and sometimes tiny amounts. **Kathleen Grimmer**, a retiree in Cedar Lake, Indiana, appears in the principal committee's Schedule A **231 separate times** this cycle, for a net total of just **$828.58** — including several small negative "CHARGEBACK" corrections (e.g. -$7.60 on 2025-09-18, `fec/C00719294/schedule_a-2026-07-20T23_36_44.csv:23283`) alongside dozens of $0.10–$2.00 charges. **Bruce Makowski**, retired, of Clawson, Michigan, shows up 198 times for a net of **$176.58** — an average of 89 cents per itemized row. Across the whole out-of-state donor pool, **30 individual donors each appear 50 or more times**, together accounting for 2,737 contribution rows (32% of the out-of-state total count) but only $34,413.24 (2% of the out-of-state dollar total) — nearly all of them retirees, spread across Indiana, Michigan, Florida, Virginia, Oregon, New Jersey, New York, Georgia, Louisiana, California, Oklahoma, Ohio, and Alabama. This pattern is overwhelmingly an out-of-state phenomenon: only **one** Texas donor (Carolyn Lohman, 72 contributions) shows this kind of high-frequency giving at all.

FEC's own data backs up part of the mechanism, if not the specific vendor: 5,940 of the 6,011 retiree rows are coded `receipt_type_desc = "EARMARKED CONTRIBUTION"` — FEC's label for money that passed through an online fundraising conduit before reaching the committee, rather than a direct check. The `conduit_committee_name` and `memo_text` fields are blank on all of these specific rows, so this report can't name the conduit for them directly — but elsewhere in the same dataset, a different earmarked contribution (an Ovintiv executive's $7,000, split across two checks) is explicitly memo-tagged **"EARMARKED (NON-DIRECTED) THROUGH WINRED"** (`fec/C00719294/schedule_a-2026-07-20T23_36_44.csv:38531`), confirming that at least some of these committees' earmarked receipts run through WinRed, the online donation-processing platform used broadly across Republican campaigns. Whether the specific retiree-heavy rows above use the same or a different conduit isn't something this report's data can confirm.

## What this actually says

1. **"33% out-of-state" and "84% out-of-state" are both true, and both are misleading on their own.** The dollar figure says Pfluger's money is still fundamentally a Texas (and specifically Permian Basin) operation. The count figure says the vast majority of individual transactions in his FEC filings are small national donations. Neither number alone captures the committees' actual donor structure — you need both, together with which committee each lives in.
2. **The JFC concentrates Texas oil money; the principal campaign concentrates the national small-dollar base.** This maps directly onto this project's prior finding (`README.md`, `reports/2026-oil-and-gas.md`) that the JFC is the vehicle for Pfluger's largest individual checks — it's also, by a wide margin, his most Texas-weighted committee by dollars.
3. **A relatively small number of habitual small-dollar retirees drive a disproportionate share of raw contribution volume.** Whatever this says about list-based small-dollar fundraising in general, it means any "number of contributions" statistic about this candidate — including the headline 84.2% figure above — is sensitive to a donor base that looks nothing like the checkbook-writing oil executives who dominate the dollar totals.
4. **The energy-industry pattern documented elsewhere in this project extends past Texas's borders.** Ovintiv, Marathon Petroleum, Cheniere, Phillips 66, ExxonMobil, and KochPAC are all headquartered outside Texas but are oil-and-gas industry money nonetheless — a reminder that "out-of-state" and "outside the industry" are not the same question.
5. **A few out-of-state donors don't fit any obvious pattern** — a California tribal gaming nation, a small Massachusetts-based ideological PAC — and are flagged here as leads for further reporting rather than conclusions.

## Caveats

This report uses the same donor-receipt scoping this project's other Pfluger reports rely on: only rows the FEC itself labels "Contributions From Individuals/Persons Other Than Political Committees" or "Contributions From Other Political Committees" count as outside money coming in (excluding inter-committee transfers and JFC pass-through attribution, per `analyze-candidate.rb`'s documented gotchas), and memo-coded rollup rows are excluded to avoid double-counting. "Out-of-state" is drawn directly from the `contributor_state` field as filed — it reflects the address the donor gave the campaign, not necessarily their current residence, employer location, or any deeper notion of geographic "connection" to the money. Eight contributions ($13,850.00) have a blank state field and are excluded from both percentages rather than assigned to either side. The retiree/small-dollar analysis is based on the free-text `contributor_occupation` field exactly as filed by the committee — "RETIRED" is the filer's own characterization, not independently verified. As with every report in this project: a legal, fully disclosed contribution is not evidence of anything improper; this report counts and traces the money and leaves judgment about what it means to the reader.
