# TX-11: August Pfluger — Out-of-State Money, 2020–2026

*What share of Pfluger's donor receipts — by dollar value AND by raw contribution count — has come from outside Texas across every cycle he's run, and who's behind it.*

## The short version

Since August Pfluger's first campaign filing in 2019, his four committees have taken in **$17,209,793.54** in itemized donor receipts (23,957 contributions total). Of that, **$4,610,838.69 — 26.8% of the money — has come from outside Texas**, but out-of-state donors account for **16,202 of the 23,957 contributions — 67.6% of every single itemized transaction filed**. (A residual $37,775.00 across 24 contributions has a blank contributor-state field and is excluded from both percentages; see Caveats.)

That gap — roughly a quarter of the dollars, two-thirds of the paperwork — is two structurally different donor pools sitting inside the same disclosures: a small number of very large Texas checks, mostly oil-and-gas money already documented in this project's `2026-oil-and-gas.md` report, and a much larger, geographically scattered base of small, repeat, often sub-$10 contributions from retirees around the country. Both are real, both are legal, and neither is visible if you look only at a top-donor list or only at a dollar total.

**The gap has also widened every single cycle.** In Pfluger's first race (2020), out-of-state money was a modest 15.4% of dollars and 14.8% of contribution count — a genuinely Texas-centric campaign. By the current 2026 cycle, out-of-state money is 33.4% of dollars and **84.4% of every contribution** — a complete structural shift in how the campaign raises money, driven almost entirely by the small-dollar national donor base described below.

## Two different money machines, one committee network

Pfluger's money runs through four committees — **August Pfluger for Congress** (his principal committee, C00719294), **Raptor PAC** (his leadership PAC, C00749481), the **Pfluger Victory Committee** (his current joint fundraising committee, or JFC, C00753913), and **PFriends of Pfluger** (an earlier, smaller JFC active 2023–2025, C00840033) — and the Texas/out-of-state split looks completely different in each one, across the whole career:

|---|---|---|---|
| August Pfluger for Congress | $5,962,080.25 (20,553 contributions) | $3,431,255.19 (57.6%) / 5,220 (25.4%) | $2,524,300.06 (42.4%) / **15,327 (74.6%)** |
| Raptor PAC | $567,622.50 (170 contributions) | $337,925.00 (59.5%) / 85 (50.0%) | $229,697.50 (40.5%) / 85 (50.0%) |
| Pfluger Victory Committee (JFC) | $10,575,590.79 (3,187 contributions) | $8,740,499.66 (82.9%) / 2,413 (76.1%) | $1,803,841.13 (17.1%) / 756 (23.9%) |
| PFriends of Pfluger (earlier JFC) | $104,500.00 (47 contributions) | $51,500.00 (49.3%) / 13 (27.7%) | $53,000.00 (50.7%) / 34 (72.3%) |

Two takeaways sit right in that table, and a third emerges once the same breakdown is run per cycle (below):

1. **Nearly all of the out-of-state contribution *count* (15,327 of 16,202, 94.6%) runs through the principal campaign committee**, not either JFC. That's the small-dollar, high-frequency donor base described below.
2. **The JFC — the vehicle for the big checks — is Pfluger's most Texas-weighted committee by dollars (82.9%)**, consistent with what this project's `2026-oil-and-gas.md` report has already found: Permian Basin oil money, concentrated in a handful of huge checks.
3. **The principal committee's own out-of-state dollar share has exploded over time** — from 16.3% in 2020 to 69.3% in 2024 and 69.2% in 2026 — while the JFC has stayed majority-Texas throughout every cycle it's existed (89.3% in 2020 rising only as high as 96.3% in 2022 before settling at 72.2% in 2026). The principal committee is where the structural shift documented above is actually happening.

## The trend, cycle by cycle

| Cycle | Total receipts | Texas ($ / n) | Out-of-state ($ / n) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 (first race) | $2,532,144.53 (1,469 contributions) | 84.6% / 85.2% | 15.4% / 14.8% |
| 2022 | $4,269,147.17 (3,331 contributions) | 81.0% / 61.4% | 18.7% / 38.3% |
| 2024 | $5,090,743.41 (8,809 contributions) | 67.5% / 32.0% | 32.3% / 67.9% |
| 2026 | $5,317,758.43 (10,348 contributions) | 66.3% / 15.6% | 33.4% / 84.4% |

The dollar-share shift is real but gradual — Texas still supplies roughly two-thirds of the money in every cycle. The *count*-share shift is dramatic: out-of-state contributions went from about one in seven of all itemized transactions in 2020 to more than four in five by 2026. That's not four times as many wealthy out-of-state donors writing checks — it's the arrival, cycle over cycle, of a large national base of small, repeat online donations, detailed below.

## The other 16,000 contributions: a nationwide base of small, repeat, retiree donors

The dollar figures above only explain a fraction of the **16,202 out-of-state contributions** on record. The volume comes from somewhere else entirely, and it's a genuinely striking pattern once isolated: of the **14,871** out-of-state contributions attributed to individual donors (as opposed to PACs), **12,512 — 84.1% — list "RETIRED" as the donor's occupation**, and together they total just **$366,380.29**. Put another way: well over half of *every single itemized contribution* Pfluger's four committees have recorded since 2019 — 15,640 rows, from Texas and everywhere else combined — is a small donation from someone who told the FEC they're retired.

These aren't one-time gifts. The same donors give over and over, in small and sometimes tiny amounts, across multiple cycles. **Bruce Makowski**, a retiree in Clawson, Michigan, appears across the principal committee's Schedule A **591 separate times** since 2020, for a net total of just **$561.27** — an average of 95 cents per row (`fec/C00719294/schedule_a-2026-08-15T11_54_12.csv:7297`, one of many). **Kathleen Grimmer**, a retiree in Cedar Lake, Indiana, appears **249 times** for a net of **$958.38**, including several small negative "CHARGEBACK" corrections (e.g. -$7.60 on 2025-09-18, `:23346`) alongside dozens of $0.10–$2.00 charges. Across the whole out-of-state donor pool, **64 individual donors each appear 50 or more times**, together accounting for 6,411 contribution rows but only $87,419.64 in total dollars — nearly all of them retirees, spread across Michigan, Wyoming, Indiana, Tennessee, Florida, and dozens of other states. This pattern is overwhelmingly an out-of-state phenomenon: only **9** Texas donors show this kind of high-frequency giving at all (918 rows combined, $33,992.57), against 64 out-of-state donors doing the same thing at more than seven times the row count.

FEC's own data confirms the mechanism directly: of the 12,512 out-of-state retiree rows, **12,335 (98.6%)** carry an "EARMARKED" `receipt_type_desc`, and **12,325 of those (98.5% of all OOS retiree rows)** explicitly name the conduit in the row's own `receipt_type_full` field — **"EARMARKED (NON-DIRECTED) THROUGH WINRED."** Bruce Makowski's own rows are a representative example (`fec/C00719294/schedule_a-2026-08-15T11_54_12.csv:7297`, a one-cent 2024-10-28 contribution carrying that exact label). WinRed is the online donation-processing platform used broadly across Republican campaigns — and it accounts for the overwhelming majority of this small-dollar, high-frequency donor base, not just an isolated example.

## What this actually says

1. **"27% out-of-state" and "68% out-of-state" are both true, and both are misleading on their own.** The dollar figure says Pfluger's money is still fundamentally a Texas (and specifically Permian Basin) operation. The count figure says the vast majority of individual transactions in his FEC filings are small national donations. Neither number alone captures the committees' actual donor structure — you need both, together with which committee and which cycle each lives in.
2. **The structural shift is recent, and it's concentrated in the principal campaign committee.** Pfluger's first two cycles (2020, 2022) looked like a conventional, mostly-in-state congressional campaign. Starting in 2024, the principal committee's donor base transformed into a majority-out-of-state, majority-small-dollar operation, while the JFC kept doing what JFCs do: concentrate a smaller number of very large, mostly-Texas checks.
3. **A relatively small number of habitual small-dollar retirees drive a disproportionate share of raw contribution volume.** Whatever this says about list-based small-dollar fundraising in general, it means any "number of contributions" statistic about this candidate — including the headline 67.6%/84.4% figures above — is sensitive to a donor base that looks nothing like the checkbook-writing oil executives who dominate the dollar totals.
4. **The energy-industry pattern documented elsewhere in this project extends past Texas's borders.** Ovintiv, Marathon Petroleum, Occidental, ExxonMobil, and KochPAC are all headquartered outside Texas but are oil-and-gas industry money nonetheless — a reminder that "out-of-state" and "outside the industry" are not the same question.
5. **A few out-of-state donors don't fit any obvious pattern** — a California tribal gaming nation, a Tampa gift-vendor-sounding LLC giving $55,000 — and are flagged here as leads for further reporting rather than conclusions.

## Caveats

This report uses the same donor-receipt scoping this project's other Pfluger reports rely on: only rows the FEC itself labels "Contributions From Individuals/Persons Other Than Political Committees" or "Contributions From Other Political Committees" count as outside money coming in, and memo-coded rollup rows are excluded to avoid double-counting. "Out-of-state" is drawn directly from the `contributor_state` field as filed — it reflects the address the donor gave the campaign, not necessarily their current residence, employer location, or any deeper notion of geographic "connection" to the money. 24 contributions ($37,775.00) have a blank state field across the full history and are excluded from both percentages rather than assigned to either side. The retiree/small-dollar analysis is based on the free-text `contributor_occupation` field exactly as filed by the committee — "RETIRED" is the filer's own characterization, not independently verified. As with every report in this project: a legal, fully disclosed contribution is not evidence of anything improper; this report counts and traces the money and leaves judgment about what it means to the reader.
