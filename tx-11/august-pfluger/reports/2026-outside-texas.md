# TX-11: August Pfluger — Out-of-State Money, 2020–2026

*What share of Pfluger's donor receipts, by dollar value and by raw contribution count, comes from outside Texas across every cycle he has run, and who's behind it.*

## The short version

Since August Pfluger's first campaign filing in 2019, his four committees have taken in **$17,659,740.61** in itemized donor receipts, across 25,461 contributions. Of that, **$4,866,956.16 (27.6% of the money) comes from outside Texas**. But out-of-state donors account for **17,424 of the 25,461 contributions: 68.4% of every itemized transaction filed**. A further $37,775.00 across 24 contributions has a blank state field. Those rows count toward the total but toward neither side; see Caveats.

That gap, roughly a quarter of the dollars against two-thirds of the paperwork, reflects two very different donor pools inside the same disclosures:
- **A small number of very large Texas checks,** mostly the oil-and-gas money documented in this project's `2026-oil-and-gas.md` report.
- **A much larger, geographically scattered base** of small, repeat, often sub-$10 contributions from retirees around the country.

Both are real, and both are legal. Neither is visible if you look only at a top-donor list or only at a dollar total.

**The gap has widened in every cycle.** In Pfluger's first race (2020), out-of-state money was a modest 15.4% of dollars and 14.8% of contributions, a genuinely Texas-centered campaign. In the current 2026 cycle, out-of-state money is 35.2% of dollars and **84.0% of every contribution**. That's a complete change in how the campaign raises money, driven almost entirely by the small-dollar national donor base described below.

## Two different money machines, one committee network

Pfluger's money runs through four committees:
- **August Pfluger for Congress**, his principal committee (C00719294);
- **Raptor PAC**, his leadership PAC (C00749481);
- the **Pfluger Victory Committee**, his current joint fundraising committee, or JFC (C00753913);
- **Pfriends of Pfluger**, an earlier, smaller JFC (C00840033).

The Texas/out-of-state split looks completely different in each one, across his whole career:

|---|---|---|---|
| August Pfluger for Congress | $6,070,127.32 (21,895 contributions) | $3,460,184.79 (57.0%) / 5,421 (24.8%) | $2,603,417.53 (42.9%) / **16,468 (75.2%)** |
| Raptor PAC | $567,622.50 (170 contributions) | $337,925.00 (59.5%) / 85 (50.0%) | $229,697.50 (40.5%) / 85 (50.0%) |
| Pfluger Victory Committee (JFC) | $10,917,490.79 (3,349 contributions) | $8,905,399.66 (81.6%) / 2,494 (74.5%) | $1,980,841.13 (18.1%) / 837 (25.0%) |
| Pfriends of Pfluger (earlier JFC) | $104,500.00 (47 contributions) | $51,500.00 (49.3%) / 13 (27.7%) | $53,000.00 (50.7%) / 34 (72.3%) |

Two findings stand out in that table, and a third appears once the same breakdown is run by cycle:

1. **Nearly all of the out-of-state contribution *count* runs through the principal campaign committee:** 16,468 of 17,424, or 94.5%. Neither JFC carries much of it. That's the small-dollar, high-frequency donor base described below.
2. **The JFC, the vehicle for the big checks, is Pfluger's most Texas-weighted committee by dollars (81.6%).** That's consistent with the `2026-oil-and-gas.md` report's finding of Permian Basin oil money concentrated in a handful of huge checks.
3. **The principal committee's own out-of-state dollar share has soared.** It went from 16.3% in 2020 to 69.2% in 2024 and 69.4% in 2026. The JFC, by contrast, is majority-Texas in every cycle it has existed: 89.3% in 2020, a peak of 95.8% in 2022, and 70.4% in 2026, its least Texas-weighted cycle so far. The principal committee is where the change documented above is happening.

## The trend, cycle by cycle

| Cycle | Total receipts | Texas ($ / n) | Out-of-state ($ / n) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 (first race) | $2,532,144.53 (1,469 contributions) | 84.6% / 85.2% | 15.4% / 14.8% |
| 2022 | $4,269,147.17 (3,331 contributions) | 81.0% / 61.4% | 18.7% / 38.3% |
| 2024 | $5,090,743.41 (8,809 contributions) | 67.5% / 32.0% | 32.3% / 67.9% |
| 2026 | $5,767,705.50 (11,852 contributions) | 64.5% / 16.0% | 35.2% / 84.0% |

The dollar-share shift is real but gradual; Texas still supplies roughly two-thirds of the money in every cycle. The *count*-share shift is dramatic. Out-of-state contributions went from about one in seven itemized transactions in 2020 to more than four in five in 2026. That's not four times as many wealthy out-of-state donors writing checks. It's the arrival, cycle after cycle, of a large national base of small, repeat online donations, detailed below.

## The other 17,000 contributions: a nationwide base of small, repeat, retiree donors

The dollar figures above explain only a fraction of the **17,424 out-of-state contributions** on record. The volume comes from somewhere else entirely, and the pattern is striking once isolated. Of the **16,010** out-of-state contributions attributed to individual donors (rather than PACs), **13,511 (84.4%)** list "RETIRED" as the donor's occupation. Together those rows total just **$394,031.97**. Counting Texas too, **16,810 rows, or 66.0% of every itemized contribution** Pfluger's four committees have recorded since 2019, are donations from someone who told the FEC they are retired.

These aren't one-time gifts. The same donors give over and over, in small and sometimes tiny amounts, across multiple cycles:
- **Bruce Makowski**, a retiree in Clawson, Mich., appears in the principal committee's receipts **642 separate times** since 2020, for a net total of just **$602.41**: an average of 94 cents per row. One example is a one-cent contribution on 2024-10-28 (`fec/C00719294/schedule_a-2026-10-18T10_20_55.csv:7297`).
- **Kathleen Grimmer**, a retiree in Cedar Lake, Ind., appears **252 times** for a net of **$970.38**. Her rows include small negative "CHARGEBACK" corrections, such as −$7.60 on 2025-09-18 (`:33992`), alongside dozens of charges between 10 cents and $2.00.

Across the whole out-of-state donor pool, **71 individual donors each appear 50 or more times**. Together they account for 7,166 contribution rows but only $104,104.47. Sixty-seven of the 71 are retirees, spread across Florida, California, Virginia, Georgia, Michigan, Wyoming, Indiana, Tennessee and dozens of other states.

The pattern is overwhelmingly out-of-state. Only **12** Texas donors show this kind of high-frequency giving (1,125 rows combined, $37,458.88), against 71 out-of-state donors with more than six times the row count.

The FEC's own data confirms the mechanism directly. Of the 12,512 out-of-state retiree rows in the processed FEC exports:
- **12,335 (98.6%)** carry an "EARMARKED" `receipt_type_desc`;
- **12,325 (98.5%)** name the conduit in the row's own `receipt_type_full` field: **"EARMARKED (NON-DIRECTED) THROUGH WINRED."**

Bruce Makowski's one-cent row cited above is a typical example. WinRed is the online donation-processing platform used widely by Republican campaigns, and it accounts for the overwhelming majority of this small-dollar, high-frequency donor base. The remaining 999 out-of-state retiree rows come from raw third-quarter 2026 filings, which don't carry that field.

## What this says

1. **"28% out-of-state" and "68% out-of-state" are both true, and each is misleading on its own.**
   - The dollar figure says Pfluger's money is still fundamentally a Texas, and specifically Permian Basin, operation.
   - The count figure says most individual transactions in his FEC filings are small national donations.

   Neither number alone captures the committees' donor structure. You need both, along with which committee and which cycle each lives in.
2. **The change is recent, and it's concentrated in the principal campaign committee.** Pfluger's first cycle looked like a conventional, mostly in-state congressional campaign. From 2022 on, and decisively from 2024, the principal committee's donor base became a majority-out-of-state, majority-small-dollar operation. Meanwhile the JFC kept doing what JFCs do: collecting a smaller number of very large, mostly Texas checks.
3. **A relatively small number of habitual small-dollar retirees drive a large share of raw contribution volume.** Whatever this says about list-based small-dollar fundraising in general, it means any "number of contributions" statistic about this candidate, including the headline 68.4% and 84.0% figures above, depends heavily on a donor base that looks nothing like the checkbook-writing oil executives who dominate the dollar totals.
4. **The energy-industry pattern documented elsewhere in this project extends past Texas's borders.** Ovintiv, Marathon Petroleum, Occidental, ExxonMobil and KochPAC are all headquartered outside Texas, but they are oil-and-gas money nonetheless. "Out-of-state" and "outside the industry" are not the same question.
5. **A few out-of-state donors don't fit any obvious pattern.** A California tribal gaming nation and a Tampa company called Cool Master Pro giving $55,000 are flagged here as leads for further reporting, not conclusions.

## Caveats

This report uses the same donor-receipt scope as this project's other Pfluger reports. Only rows the FEC itself labels "Contributions From Individuals/Persons Other Than Political Committees" or "Contributions From Other Political Committees" count as outside money coming in. Memo-coded rollup rows are excluded to avoid double-counting.

"Out-of-state" comes directly from the `contributor_state` field as filed. It reflects the address the donor gave the campaign, not necessarily their current residence, employer location, or any deeper "connection" to the money. Across the full history, 24 contributions ($37,775.00) have a blank state field. They count toward the denominators but toward neither side, so Texas and out-of-state percentages sum to slightly less than 100%.

The retiree/small-dollar analysis relies on the free-text `contributor_occupation` field exactly as filed. "RETIRED" is the filer's own description, not independently verified.

As with every report in this project, a legal, fully disclosed contribution is not evidence of anything improper. This report counts and traces the money and leaves judgment about what it means to the reader.
