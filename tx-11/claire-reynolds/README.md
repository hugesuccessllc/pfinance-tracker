# TX-11: Claire Reynolds — Financial Disclosure Summary (2026 Cycle)

Claire Reynolds, a Democrat from Austin, is running in Texas's 11th Congressional District against Republican incumbent August Pfluger. The redrawn TX-11 joins the district's historic West Texas core (Midland, Odessa, San Angelo) with a strip of North Austin, most of Pflugerville, and parts of Round Rock, Cedar Park, and Leander.

This summary covers her principal campaign committee, **Claire Reynolds for Congress (C00929711)**, from its first itemized transaction on November 30, 2025 through September 30, 2026, the close of the third-quarter reporting period. It also draws on her House candidate financial disclosure, filed May 11, 2026.

**The short version:** this is a small-dollar, volunteer-scale campaign. It raised **$122,571.79** and spent **$69,048.56**, leaving **$53,523.23** in the bank on September 30. More than a quarter of its money came from donors giving $200 or less. It has no payroll, and its budget is dominated by yard signs and printed mail.

## 3. Takeaways

**1. This is a small-donor campaign, and the money gap with the incumbent is enormous.** Small gifts of $200 or less are 28.5% of Reynolds's individual contributions, more than her top 10 donors combined. For scale against her $89,300 in itemized contributions, the same tool run on data collected for August Pfluger on August 15, 2026 shows $642,408 in itemized contributions to his principal campaign committee alone and $2.6 million in itemized spending by it. His joint fundraising committee, the Pfluger Victory Fund, shows another $4.6 million in itemized receipts. (No FEC summary totals are collected for Pfluger, so his unitemized money isn't known here. Itemized-to-itemized is the fair comparison.) The Reynolds campaign is running a yard-sign-and-volunteer race against a multimillion-dollar operation. The spending pattern reflects that: no payroll, no TV, and almost no paid digital. The $53,523 still in the bank on September 30 is the main open question. It could fund a late push, or it could go unspent.

**2. The campaign's geography has two centers, and that matches the district.** Donors and vendors are concentrated in Austin, which the new map brought into TX-11. Travel spending, field organizing, and a handful of notable donors (Hogg in San Angelo, Ketzenberger in Odessa, Crosby in Ballinger) point to deliberate work in the district's West Texas core. The most out-of-place money is the San Diego cluster, roughly a fifth of all itemized dollars. It reads like a personal network rather than an organized national donor base.

**3. Her personal finances are modest by congressional standards, with no liabilities and no oil and gas stocks.** Her House candidate report (Filing ID 10076507, covering January 1, 2025 to April 15, 2026) lists 50 asset entries with reported values totaling between about **$715,000 and $2.06 million**. It discloses no liabilities, no outside positions, and no agreements.
   - The largest single asset is the household's 50% interest in Huge Success, LLC, which holds a rental property in Terlingua (Brewster County) valued at $250,001–$500,000. It produced $5,001–$15,000 in rent in 2025.
   - She reports $120,000 in 2025 salary from the State Bar of Texas, and $0 from that source for 2026 through the filing date.
   - Most of the rest is retirement savings, hers and her husband's, plus his vested runZero stock options ($50,001–$100,000).
   - The individually named stocks lean heavily toward technology and cybersecurity: CrowdStrike, Rapid7, Cloudflare, Snowflake, Microsoft, ASML. None is an oil and gas company. That's notable in a district whose West Texas core is the Permian Basin.

**Committees analyzed:** C00929711 (Claire Reynolds for Congress, principal). No other committees are collected under `tx-11/claire-reynolds/fec/`, and none is marked CONNECTED.

**Summary totals:** the totals for raised, spent, cash on hand, unitemized gifts, and debts come from `fec/C00929711/fec-summary-2026-10-12.txt`. **EFILE COVERAGE WARNING:** the tool reported that fec.gov's processed exports for C00929711 end on June 30, 2026, while the raw efile data runs through September 30, 2026: the Q3 report, filed October 10, 2026. Per gotcha 8 in the tool's header, the Q3 rows were folded into every total above. That added 96 receipt rows ($23,843.88) and 59 disbursement rows ($25,843.66). **Data-integrity checks that changed findings:**
- **Gap-filled committee receipts.** The tool previously counted gap-filled line-11C receipts only when the entity type was "PAC". That dropped the $1,000 Texas Democratic Women of Highland Lakes contribution, which was filed as "PTY".
- **Split payees.** Payee totals were also split by letter case between processed rows ("PRINTMAIL PRO") and efile rows ("PrintMail Pro").
- Both bugs were fixed in `tooling/analyze-candidate.rb` this session and are documented as gotcha 11 in its header.
- **Unitemized money.** The tool also gained the summary-file reconciliation described above (gotcha 12). Without it, raised and spent totals read only from the CSV exports understate fundraising by 27% and spending by 8%.
- Memo-coded Schedule A rows (ActBlue conduit duplicates of real contributions) and candidate in-kind memos were excluded from totals, per gotcha 1.
- The per-election limit check above was done by hand from each row's primary/general designation (`election_type` / `pgo`).

**Rerun note:** this run reflected a 2026-10-12 data refresh that added the Q3 2026 filing and FEC summary totals, together with the tooling fixes described above.

**District geography** (the description of the redrawn TX-11) is context supplied by the repository owner. It is not derived from the FEC or House data. No boundary claims here were checked against the shapefiles in `district-maps/`.

### Caveats

- **Donor and payee detail covers itemized money only.** The $33,189.22 from small donors and $5,319.02 in small payments have no names, addresses, or dates, so the donor lists, geography, and spending categories describe the itemized portion. Debt totals come from the summary, but who is owed requires Schedule D, which isn't collected.
- **Individual payees in the Q3 efile appear by surname only** ("Young", "Ehresman"), because the raw efile disbursement export has a single name field. First names will appear once fec.gov's processed export catches up.
- **Spending categories are this report's own grouping** by payee and description. They are not FEC category codes, which the committee left blank.
- **House disclosure values are ranges set by law.** The $715,000–$2.06 million figure sums each asset's lower and upper bounds and is not a precise net worth. An earlier filing in the same directory (`8221333.pdf`) is image-only, has no extractable text, and is superseded by the signed 10076507 report used here.

**Prompt history (v8):** Several `tx-11/claire-reynolds/reports/` write-ups regenerated in the same session as a data refresh or a tooling fix (a keyword-scan expansion, an efile-gap bug fix) kept spending a chunk of the new report explaining how the new numbers differed from the old ones — "the earlier version found $X, this version found $Y" — right in the body a reader would see. That's a changelog, not a report, and it's exactly the instinct "A standing rule: reports are not diffs" (above, under "Step 2: Analyze") now exists to head off. v8 doesn't change anything about how `analyze-candidate.rb` is run; it adds one instruction to this prompt's own Methodology section (below) making that standing rule concrete for this specific template, so a fresh session re-running this prompt for a new cycle — or a user regenerating an existing report after a fix — doesn't have to be told twice.

**Prompt history (v7):** A v6 run on Claire Reynolds's committee (TX-11, C00929711) reported her donor/spending activity as ending 2026-03-31, silently missing an entire second quarter (April–June 2026) that the campaign had already filed. The cause: `analyze-candidate.rb` only ever read `schedule_a-*.csv`/`schedule_b-*.csv` — fec.gov's "processed" bulk exports — and those can lag well behind what's actually been filed, sometimes by months, with no warning that they're stale. The raw `efile-*.csv` data sitting in the same directory (from clicking "Raw data" during Step 1's manual export, or from `fec-api-client.rb`'s automated download) already had the missing quarter; the tool just never looked at it. `analyze-candidate.rb` now reads `efile-*.csv` itself — but only ever for the narrow slice of rows dated strictly after whatever date the processed schedule_a/schedule_b export already covers, and only once per committee/schedule. It never touches or re-derives anything for a period the processed files already cover, which is what keeps this safe against the double-counting risk that kept efile out of scope entirely before (v6 and earlier told this prompt to never glob `efile-*.csv` in at all — see the v6 note below for why that caution existed, and gotcha 8 in `tooling/analyze-candidate.rb`'s header for the amendment-handling and name-casing details that make gap-filling correct rather than just plausible-looking). Nothing changed in this prompt's own instructions to make this happen — v7 exists only to record the incident and point at the tool as the actual fix, per this repo's standing rule that data-integrity logic lives in `analyze-candidate.rb`'s header, not duplicated here. One thing this prompt now does ask for explicitly: if the tool's output includes an `EFILE COVERAGE WARNING` banner, say so in the Methodology section (see the updated Methodology instructions below) rather than letting it pass unremarked — the whole point of surfacing it is so a reader can tell the difference between "no more recent filings exist" and "more recent filings exist and are already reflected below."

**Prompt history (v6):** v5 restricted this prompt to the principal committee **only**, even if other committees (a JFC, a leadership PAC) were already sitting collected in `tx-11/claire-reynolds/fec/`. That was overcorrection: the actual invariant Step 2 needs to hold is "never download, itemize, or otherwise collect anything itself" — not "only ever look at one committee." A user (or an LLM doing Step 1 as its own deliberate task) might already know a candidate's JFC matters and go collect it manually before ever running this prompt — say, because they read a prior "Suggested Committees" note, or just already knew about Raptor PAC and Pfluger Victory Committee and grabbed them alongside the principal committee from the start. Refusing to use data that's already sitting on disk, correctly collected, would be perverse. v6 analyzes **everything already collected** under `tx-11/claire-reynolds/fec/` — one committee or several — and reserves "Suggested Committees for Further Investigation" for naming committees that are visible but *not yet* collected, exactly as before.

**Prompt history (v5):** v4 still had this prompt run `fec-api-client.rb --download` itself, right before the analysis step, on the theory that a "principal committee only" download was narrow enough to be safe. In practice that still meant an analysis prompt could reach for the network and decide, on its own, that data needed fetching — the same instinct that led earlier versions to auto-chase linked/affiliated committees. v5 removes the download command from this prompt entirely. Collection (Step 1) and analysis (Step 2) are now strictly separate: if the required `fec/` data isn't already on disk when this prompt runs, **stop and say so** — tell the user which committee ID(s) to collect and point them at "Step 1: Collect Your Data" above — rather than fetching it yourself. This also makes the iterative workflow explicit: fast-pass on just the principal committee, read what it suggests investigating, go collect those committees yourself (Step 1 again, deliberately), then re-run this same analysis prompt against the now-larger `fec/` directory.

**Prompt history (v4):** v3 had this prompt download the principal committee's data plus, via a `--with-affiliated` flag, another committee auto-discovered by name search (and before that, a `--with-linked` flag that recursively crawled every committee ever referenced in a Schedule B transfer). Both put the *tool* in charge of deciding which other committees mattered — which either missed the committee that actually carried most of a candidate's money (a JFC's name search can fail; see `tooling/fec-api-client.rb`'s header) or, worse, pulled in large, unrelated committees (a party committee, another candidate's committee) that happened to receive a transfer. v4 scoped this prompt to the principal committee **only**, with `analyze-candidate.rb`'s own Schedule B data (a `recipient_committee_id` for any transfer recipient that's itself a committee — zero extra API calls) feeding a "Suggested Committees for Further Investigation" output section instead of an automatic download.

**Prompt history:** the pilot run of this prompt (TX-11/August-Pfluger) shipped a summary with a "Correction (post-publication review)" section — it took a second pass, prompted by a human asking pointed questions, to catch a couple of data-integrity bugs after the fact.
