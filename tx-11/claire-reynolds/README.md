# TX-11: Claire Reynolds — Financial Disclosure Summary (2026 Cycle)

Claire Reynolds, the Democratic candidate for TX-11, runs her 2026 campaign through a single committee — **Claire Reynolds for Congress** (C00929711) — with no joint fundraising committee, leadership PAC, or other affiliated vehicle in the picture. Itemized donor receipts this cycle total **$65,455.74**, of which **92.4% ($60,455.74)** came from individuals and just **7.6% ($5,000.00)** from a single political committee. Against that, itemized disbursements total **$37,885.88**, leaving the campaign having raised roughly **$27,570** more than it has spent so far this cycle. This is a small, single-committee operation — nothing here moves through a JFC or a party account, so there's no "Transfers from authorized committees" line to untangle the way there is for better-funded incumbents. This report also folds in Reynolds's House Ethics Committee financial disclosure, a Candidate Report covering January 2025 through April 2026.

## Takeaways

1. **This is a grassroots, individually-funded campaign with essentially no institutional money.** Individuals supplied 92.4% of itemized receipts, and the only PAC or committee contribution all cycle is a single $5,000 check from Vote Save America. There's no JFC, no leadership PAC, no corporate PAC checks, and no party-committee transfers to account for — a genuinely simple funding picture compared to an incumbent running through a multi-committee network.

2. **The candidate and her spouse are personally subsidizing the campaign's smallest costs.** Reynolds fronted $4,607.01 in out-of-pocket purchases this cycle — the entire ballot-access filing fee, domain names, business cards, a WordPress template, a Progress Texas ticket — correctly excluded from both totals as in-kind contributions rather than new money. Her spouse, Tod Beardsley, is also a small direct donor ($686.26 cash plus $107.17 in IT equipment) and was reimbursed $1,070.45 for his own travel and shipping while helping the campaign — reimbursement, not compensation.

3. **Donor geography already reaches into the district's newly redrawn edges.** Austin and San Angelo dominate the donor map, as expected, but a $3,500 donor from Leander — a city the 2026 map newly folded into TX-11 — shows the campaign is already raising money from territory it didn't previously represent.

4. **Spending reads as lean and conventional, not flashy.** With 96.3% of disbursements uncategorized by the FEC's own optional field (a data-completeness gap, not a red flag), the vendor-level detail shows unremarkable challenger-campaign costs: printing and mailers, yard signs, VAN/EveryAction compliance software, a single ballot-access filing fee, Facebook ads, mileage reimbursements, and one canvassing payment. There's no equivalent here to the kind of high-end travel or dining spend that shows up in better-funded committees' books.

5. **Receipts are running well ahead of spending.** The committee has raised about $27,570 more than it has spent this cycle ($65,455.74 vs. $37,885.88), consistent with a campaign still building toward the general election rather than burning cash as fast as it comes in.

6. **The household's own financial disclosure is straightforward, with one out-of-district asset.** Reynolds's most recent reported income (calendar year 2025) was a $120,000 salary from the State Bar of Texas — a legal-sector job, not campaign income. Her spouse, Tod Beardsley, draws a salary from runZero (amount not required to be disclosed, standard for spousal earned income) plus a $1,463 honorarium for speaking at Black Hat USA, a cybersecurity conference, consistent with his industry background. Household assets are mostly retirement accounts (Reynolds's 401(k)/457 plan/Roth IRA, Beardsley's 401(k)/IRAs/brokerage account, each in the $1,000–$250,000 range) plus one jointly-held real estate holding: a $250,001–$500,000 stake in a rental property, "Casa Terlingua," held through Huge Success, LLC (50% joint ownership). Terlingua sits in Brewster County — territory that, per this repository's district-boundary research, has never been part of TX-11 under either the old or newly redrawn map. That's not a residency problem (candidates aren't required to hold property inside their district), just worth noting plainly: the household's real estate is outside the district Reynolds is running to represent. No liabilities, outside positions, or reportable agreements were disclosed.
