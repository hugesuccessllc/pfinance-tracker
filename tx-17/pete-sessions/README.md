# TX-17: Pete Sessions — Financial Disclosure Summary (2026 Cycle)

Pete Sessions' 2026-cycle fundraising runs through a single committee, **Pete Sessions for Congress 2002** (C00303305) — a long-running committee ID whose name preserves the year it was first registered, long before his current TX-17 seat. No joint fundraising committee, leadership PAC, or other affiliated entity is collected in this repository for him, and none is visible as a money source in his own Schedule A either: unlike some of his colleagues, none of his itemized 2026 receipts arrive via a "Transfers from authorized committees" line, so this report's donor total is not missing a JFC-sized chunk hiding one line down.

Itemized donor receipts for the cycle total **$1,011,080.99**, split almost exactly down the middle between individuals (**$494,435.02**, 48.9%) and PACs/other committees (**$516,645.97**, 51.1%). Itemized disbursements total **$556,408.62**. Two Periodic Transaction Reports — one in Sessions' own name, one for his spouse's IRA — round out the picture on the House Ethics side; no annual Financial Disclosure Statement is present in the collected files.

## Takeaways

1. **Donor money is genuinely balanced between individuals and PACs, and no JFC obscures the picture.** At 48.9% individual / 51.1% PAC, Sessions' fundraising doesn't lean on either side the way some colleagues' committees do — and because everything runs through one committee with no "Transfers from authorized committees" money coming in from elsewhere, this $1,011,080.99 figure is the complete itemized-receipts picture for 2026, not a partial one waiting on an uncollected JFC.

2. **A modest but real network-building operation runs straight out of the principal committee — no separate leadership PAC required.** $58,000 (29 checks, mostly $2,000 apiece) went to other Republican House candidates' committees nationally: Miller-Meeks (IA), Ciscomani (AZ-06), Valadao (CA), Kiggans (VA), Lawler (NY-17), Kean (NJ), Boebert (CO), Calvert (CA), John James (MI), and roughly a dozen more first-time or competitive-seat candidates (Briscoe Cain, Gabe Evans, Tom Barrett, and others). Unlike a member who routes this kind of giving through a dedicated leadership PAC, Sessions writes these checks directly from his own campaign account.

3. **Two of those 29 checks went to fellow comeback bids by out-of-office Republicans.** The transfer-recipient list includes $2,000 to **Van Hilleary for Congress** — a former Tennessee congressman (1995–2003) whose committee name suggests a bid to return to the House — and $2,000 to **Paul LePage for Congress**, the former two-term governor of Maine, whose committee (C00904094) shows him running for a U.S. House seat. Sessions himself previously represented TX-32 for over two decades before losing re-election in 2018, then won TX-17 in 2020 — so a check to two other Republicans attempting their own returns to elected office reads less like a coincidence and more like an affinity. Notably, a $2,416.53 Bar Harbor Inn charge — Bar Harbor, Maine, nowhere near Texas — shows up in the same disbursement data as the LePage check; the filing doesn't establish a direct link between the two, but the geographic coincidence is worth flagging for anyone tracking Sessions' travel.

4. **The card-processor spending includes other travel far outside the district, too.** Beyond the Maine charge, Trump National Doral (Florida) shows up as a $4,301.98 itemized sub-transaction behind the Aviator/Amex lump payments — alongside the more explicable Capital Grille tab in Washington, DC.

5. **Industry giving tilts toward aviation and financial services rather than a single dominant sector.** Allied Pilots Association, National Air Traffic Controllers Association, UBS Americas, and Ernst & Young all gave five-figure or near-five-figure sums — a spread that fits a member without one overwhelming local industry patron, rather than the oil-and-gas-heavy donor base seen in some other Texas delegations.

6. **The House Ethics record on file is thin: two stock trades, no annual disclosure.** Both collected filings are Periodic Transaction Reports, not the annual Financial Disclosure Statement — see Methodology for what that does and doesn't imply.

- **Command run:**

    --fec-dir tx-17/pete-sessions/fec \
    --house-ethics-dir tx-17/pete-sessions/house-ethics \

- **Data provenance:** C00303305's directory carries `.download-progress` and `.efile-progress` marker files (status: complete), indicating collection via `fec-api-client.rb --download` rather than manual export; the `schedule_a`/`schedule_b` CSVs are timestamped 2026-09-11, with two supplementary `efile-*.csv` files timestamped 2026-07-15 and 2026-07-28. The two House Ethics PDFs carry no such markers and were collected manually.
- **A tool bug surfaced and fixed during this run:** `analyze-candidate.rb`'s processed-export `is_individual` check only ever matched the single-letter FEC bulk code `"t"`, but this committee's schedule_a export spells the same boolean out as the full words `"true"`/`"false"` instead — a format variation that, it turns out, also affects one of August Pfluger's committees (C00857649) and Casey Shepard's TX-17 committee, though not most other committees already in this repository. Left unfixed, every donor row in this file would have read as `false` (committee/PAC), because `"true" != "t"`, which is exactly what the first run of this analysis showed: a misleading 100% committee/PAC, 0% individual split. This is now documented as gotcha 10 in the tool's header, fixed via a shared `individual_flag?` helper that accepts both spellings, and covered by a new spec case; re-running the analysis after the fix produced the 48.9% individual / 51.1% committee split reported above. No other total in this report (donor amounts, disbursements, category breakdowns) was affected — `is_individual` is only used for this one classification, not for summing.
- **Cycle integrity warning:** the tool flagged 455 rows (within the 2026-scoped data) where `fec_election_year` disagrees with `two_year_transaction_period`. Spot-checking the flagged transaction IDs shows these are legitimate: several donors (e.g. the Howard, Livanios, and Neuman households in the Key Donors table) gave in a single batch that was simultaneously designated across four election types — 2026 primary, 2026 general, 2028 primary, and 2028 general — which the FEC records as one `two_year_transaction_period` (2026, when the money was received) against a mix of `fec_election_year` values (2026 and 2028, which election each designated portion counts toward). This is a normal pattern for donors giving toward multiple future elections at once, not a data error, and doesn't change any total above since `--cycle 2026` correctly scopes by `two_year_transaction_period`, not `fec_election_year`.
- **EFILE COVERAGE WARNING:** not triggered. The processed `schedule_a`/`schedule_b` exports (timestamped 2026-09-11) already extend past the two supplementary `efile-*.csv` files' own date ranges (July 2026 exports), so no gap-filling was needed and none of the figures above rely on raw efile data.
- **House Ethics filings:** both collected PDFs are Periodic Transaction Reports (PTRs), not the annual Financial Disclosure Statement — Filing #20033500 (filed 2026-03-09): a sale of Verizon Communications common stock (VZ), $1,001–$15,000, held directly by Sessions; Filing #20035068 (filed 2026-07-24): a sale of Ares Capital Corporation closed-end fund shares (ARCC), $1,001–$15,000, held by Sessions' spouse in an IRA. Neither report indicates capital gains over $200. No annual Financial Disclosure Statement is present in the collected `house-ethics/` directory — per this repository's standing note that House Ethics filings are legally required, an absent filing type in a candidate's directory more often reflects a Step 1 collection gap than a candidate who didn't file, and that possibility hasn't been ruled out here.
