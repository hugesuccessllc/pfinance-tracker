# TX-19: Kyle Rable — Financial Disclosure Summary (2026 Cycle)

**Bottom line:** Kyle Rable's campaign, Rable for West Texas (C00900514), is a very small, small-donor operation. From March 2025 through September 30, 2026 it raised **$46,240** and spent **$31,418**, and it reported **$11,989 in cash on hand with no debts or loans**. Nearly half of the money (about $21,400) came from donors giving $200 or less, so no single supporter is large enough to show up in the line-item data. Rable's own House financial disclosures show a modest personal picture: a graduate teaching stipend, Army Reserve drill pay, a spouse's city salary, no assets listed, and a student-loan debt in the $100,001–$250,000 range.

A note on how to read the numbers: FEC line-item exports only list donors who gave more than $200 in the cycle and vendors paid more than $200. The campaign's own summary page covers everything. Where this report gives a "total" it uses the summary page; where it names donors and payees it uses the line items.

## Takeaways

1. **The donor base is broader than the donor list suggests.** 46% of the money came from donors giving $200 or less, so the ten names above account for roughly a third of the total raised. A campaign this small can have a long tail of modest supporters and still rely on a handful of Lubbock-area friends for its larger checks.

2. **Labor is the only organized money.** The two checks from organizations came from unions, and one of them, the Communications Workers of America, also received a $1,048 payment from the campaign for an event. The two transactions are filed separately and nothing in the data connects them. There is no corporate PAC money anywhere.

3. **The campaign is spending on the basics.** Stickers, mailers, texting, a website, voter data and gas make up nearly all of the spending. There is no consultant retainer, polling firm or staff payroll in the data, which fits a volunteer-run effort but makes the campaign's capacity hard to compare with better-funded races.

4. **Raising money is picking up late.** Itemized receipts ran about $1,000–$3,000 a month through spring 2026, then the third-quarter filing added another $8,870, the largest single quarter in the data. The campaign still held $11,989 at the end of September and owed nothing.

5. **The candidate has little personal money to put in, and has put in little.** His disclosures show earned income of a few tens of thousands of dollars in his own name, a spouse's salary of about $50,000, no listed assets and six-figure student debt. His campaign contributions total about $500, so the campaign runs on other people's money.
