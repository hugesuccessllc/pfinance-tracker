# TX-19: Tom Sell — Financial Disclosure Summary (2026 Cycle)

**Bottom line:** Tom Sell's campaign, Conservative Tom Sell for Congress (C00926345), raised **$2.39 million** between October 1, 2025 and September 30, 2026, spent **$2.10 million**, and ended the quarter with **$285,172 in cash** and $26,170 in unpaid bills. There are no loans and no candidate contributions. The money came from two places: wealthy Texas agriculture and crop-insurance families giving the legal maximum, and a long list of farm-sector political action committees (PACs). More than half of everything the campaign itemized as spending, **$1.15 million**, went to a single Richmond, Virginia media-buying firm.

Sell's own House financial disclosures add context the campaign filings don't: Sell is the managing member of a Lubbock consulting firm and reports being paid more than $5,000 each by 14 agriculture groups for "government affairs consulting," and at least five of those groups' names match PACs that gave to the campaign.

A note on how to read the numbers: FEC line-item exports list only donors who gave more than $200 in the cycle and vendors paid more than $200. The campaign's own summary page covers everything. Where this report gives a "total," it uses the summary page; where it names donors and payees, it uses line items.

**The joint fundraising committee.** A second committee the candidate controls, Tom Sell Victory Committee (C00953604), reported **$267,550** raised and **$253,610** spent between April 1 and September 30, 2026, ending with $13,940 in cash and $2,728 in debts. What the committee's itemized receipts show (July 26 to September 30, 2026; $267,050 across 116 rows and 115 donors, which ties to the summary page once $500 of unitemized money is added):

- **It is a September operation.** $214,050 (80%) arrived in September, $47,000 in August and $6,000 in July.
- **Gifts are large and mostly Texan.** About 91% of the money came from Texas addresses. The largest single givers were Ross Hilburn of Lubbock and Tucker Bridwell of Abilene at $17,000 each, followed by five donors at $10,000. Eight gifts were partly reattributed to spouses after the fact (for example, $8,500 of Hilburn's moved to Kelli Hilburn), so these are usually couples' money. Checks above the $3,500 single-election limit are normal here, since a joint committee splits each check among its participants.
- **PACs are a small share.** Five PACs gave $16,000 combined: Plains Cotton Growers and the Southeast Cotton Committee at $5,000 each, the Mississippi Farm Bureau and the International Association of Drilling Contractors at $2,500 each, and a First National Bank Texas committee at $1,000. A further $2,000 came from Texans for Jodey Arrington, the committee of the district's current congressman.
- **Many donors are repeats.** By name, 43 of the 115 donors, giving $138,750 through this committee, also appear in the campaign's own donor files.

## Takeaways

1. **Sell's clients and Sell's donors overlap.** The disclosure lists the Western Peanut Growers Association, USA Rice Federation, National Sorghum Producers, Minnesota Corn Growers and Crop Insurance Professionals Association as clients. PACs with those same names gave the campaign $15,000, $8,000, $10,000, $3,700 and $15,000, respectively: **about $51,700**. This is a match on names only; the filings don't say whether a client's PAC and the client are controlled by the same people, and nothing here shows the money was connected to the consulting work. But a candidate whose own income comes from lobbying agriculture groups is being funded by agriculture groups, and a voter can read that for themselves.

2. **Wealthy couples are the individual donor core.** Four couples gave $21,000 each, the top of what a household can give across three elections. The campaign's own refunds ($8,000 to Samuel Scheef, $3,000 to Tucker Bridwell) show it was catching over-limit checks, though one or two entries (Van De Pol, the Snodgrass LLC) are still unexplained.

3. **One vendor is more than half the spending.** Poolhouse Agency took $1.15 million, including a $190,030 payment on the last day of the quarter. The next-largest vendor got $286,266. Public records show who got paid but not what the ads said or how much of each payment was agency fee versus actual airtime.

4. **The campaign front-loaded and then coasted.** About $1.75 million of receipts came in by March 2026; the following summer months brought $23,000–$54,000 each. The campaign paid three consultants $47,000 in "win bonuses" on June 1–9, 2026. It ended September with $285,000 in the bank.

5. **The joint fundraising committee raised most of its money in the final month, and about $224,000 of its outflow isn't traced.** It took in $214,050 of its $267,050 in itemized receipts in September, from many of the same Texas donors who fund the campaign, and says it sent $239,637 to other committees while giving this campaign $15,870. Its receipts are itemized on disk; its disbursements are not, so who got the rest isn't known.
