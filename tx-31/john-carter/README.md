# TX-31: John Carter — Financial Disclosure Summary (2026 Cycle)

John R. Carter is the Republican incumbent in Texas's 31st Congressional District, a seat he has held since 2003. Redistricting effective for the 2026 election consolidates Fort Cavazos — previously split between TX-31 and the neighboring 11th District — entirely within TX-31, alongside all of Bell County (Killeen) and Coryell County, plus part of Williamson County (Georgetown and most of Round Rock; small pieces of Round Rock, Cedar Park, and Leander move to TX-11 instead). Two FEC committees report 2026-cycle activity: his principal campaign committee, **Judge John Carter for Congress Committee** (C00371203), and his leadership PAC, **Conservative & Republican Together Equals Results PAC** (C00427401, "CRATER PAC"). Combined, the two reported **$1,004,836.52** in itemized receipts and **$1,560,565.81** in itemized disbursements for the 2026 cycle. A third committee, **Judge Carter Victory Fund** (C00717272) — a joint fundraising committee not itself collected here — transferred **$526,755.70** into the principal committee this cycle alone, more than half again as much as everything counted in Key Donors below; see Suggested Committees for Further Investigation for why that money isn't in this report's totals.

## Takeaways

1. **A joint fundraising committee not collected here moved more money than the entire reported donor list.** Judge Carter Victory Fund (C00717272) transferred $526,755.70 into the principal committee this cycle — plus $3,041.89 from a separate "2003 Victory Fund" (C00905778) — all booked under Schedule A's "Transfers from authorized committees" line, which is correctly excluded from donor totals because it represents pooled JFC proceeds rather than a direct contributor relationship. Every one of these 10 transfer rows is attributed to the JFC itself, not to a named individual or PAC passing through it, meaning (unlike some other candidates' data) there's no evidence of earmarked donor money hiding in this line — but the sheer size, more than half again the $1,004,836.52 already reported above, means this report captures well under half of the money that actually reached Carter's committees this cycle. See Suggested Committees below.

2. **The defense-and-aerospace donor bloc tracks the district's own new geography.** Fort Cavazos is one of the largest U.S. Army installations in the country, and 2026 redistricting brings the entire post into TX-31 for the first time, ending years of it being split with the neighboring 11th District. The itemized PAC money reads accordingly: Lockheed Martin, General Dynamics, BAE Systems, Leonardo DRS, L3Harris, Textron, Boeing, Honeywell, and CACI together contributed roughly $126,000 — the largest identifiable donor bloc by industry, and a closer fit to the district's constituent economy than most incumbents' donor rolls achieve.

3. **The leadership PAC hosted fundraisers at Austin FC's stadium.** Three "EVENT - VENUE RENTAL" payments totaling $40,509.54 to the city's Major League Soccer club, spread across July 2025 and June 2026, stand out among an otherwise conventional mix of ranch venues and the Capitol Hill Club — a detail worth independent confirmation of what kind of event (a suite, a full venue buyout, a sponsorship-linked fundraiser) that spend actually bought.

4. **A single specialty-medicine PAC gave as much as any individual defense contractor.** The American Society of Anesthesiologists PAC's $20,000 combined gift is unusual both in size and in being the only major non-defense industry interest to crack the top donor tier — worth watching against any health-care-payment legislation Carter takes up.

5. **The card-payment "black box" wasn't actually opaque — it just needed a different key.** This candidate's FEC exports don't use the back-reference field the analysis tool normally relies on to unpack lump card payments, so pulling the 870 raw memo-coded rows directly (rather than relying on the standard parent/child match) was necessary to surface real spending on a private congressional club, airlines, pharmacies, grocery stores, and a consultant paid exclusively through card installments rather than direct checks — none of which would show up if a reader stopped at the top-level "Chase" line.

6. **Personal finances reveal a concentrated stock position and a decades-old student loan.** Carter's 2025 Annual Financial Disclosure (filed 2026-05-14) shows his largest asset by far is a single Edward Jones brokerage account (valued $1,000,001–$5,000,000, generating $50,001–$100,000 in dividend income) built around Exxon Mobil common stock — a notable single-issuer concentration for a sitting member of Congress. His other disclosed income comes from two Texas public pensions (Texas Judicial Retirement System and Texas County & District Retirement System), consistent with his pre-Congress career as a state district judge. On the liability side, alongside a mortgage refinanced with PNC Bank in 2024, he still lists a Mohela student loan dating to 1988 — a small, humanizing detail on an otherwise seven-figure balance sheet.

## Suggested Committees for Further Investigation

**Judge Carter Victory Fund (C00717272)** is the clear priority: this joint fundraising committee transferred $526,755.70 into the principal committee in the 2026 cycle alone (see Takeaway 1), more than half again as much as everything counted in Key Donors above. Downloading its own itemized Schedule A would surface the real individual and PAC donors behind that pooled figure — the same way pulling a JFC's own data has, for other candidates in this repository, revealed both the donors behind an earmarked line and separate transfer streams to other committees that staring at the principal committee's own Schedule A never would have shown. A smaller, related committee, **2003 Victory Fund (C00905778)**, contributed $3,041.89 the same way and could be worth a quick look alongside it, though its scale is minor by comparison.

Beyond the JFCs, this committee's own Schedule B shows small outbound transfers to several other committees (WinRed, $10,773.78; Monica for Congress, $5,000.00; Ken Calvert for Congress, $5,000.00; Valadao for Congress, $5,000.00; and others under $3,200 each) — these are money moving out, not sources of Carter's own funding, and none stand out as warranting a separate download.
