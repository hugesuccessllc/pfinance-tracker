# TX-31: John Carter — Financial Disclosure Summary (2026 Cycle)

John R. Carter is the Republican incumbent in Texas's 31st Congressional District, a seat he has held since 2003. Redistricting effective for the 2026 election consolidates Fort Cavazos — previously split between TX-31 and the neighboring 11th District — entirely within TX-31, alongside all of Bell County (Killeen) and Coryell County, plus part of Williamson County (Georgetown and most of Round Rock; small pieces of Round Rock, Cedar Park, and Leander move to TX-11 instead). Three FEC committees report 2026-cycle activity: his principal campaign committee, **Judge John Carter for Congress Committee** (C00371203); his leadership PAC, **Conservative & Republican Together Equals Results PAC** (C00427401, "CRATER PAC"); and a joint fundraising committee, **Judge Carter Victory Fund** (C00717272), that raises money jointly for Carter's own committees and the National Republican Congressional Committee (NRCC). Across all three, **$1,716,913.13** was raised from outside donors this cycle and **$1,585,054.54** was spent (excluding money simply moving between the candidate's own committees — see Methodology for how that's calculated). A fourth committee touching this data, **2003 Victory Fund**, is a separate multi-candidate joint fundraising committee that sent only 13% of what it raised to Carter; per this repository's standing policy on financially-connected-but-not-owned committees, it's excluded from the totals above (see Methodology).

Combined across all three committees, the overall split flips from PAC-heavy to individual-heavy: $1,148,102.03 individual (66.9%) against $568,811.10 PAC/committee (33.1%) — almost entirely because the JFC's donor base skews so heavily toward named individuals, many with employer listings pointing toward the defense-technology and venture-capital world (Beacon AI, HavocAI, Riot Ventures, Omni Federal, "Deterrence") rather than the traditional prime-contractor PACs that dominate the direct-committee list.

Geographically, direct-committee donors lean Beltway: Virginia ($227,100.00, 110 contributions) and Washington, DC ($199,650.00, 98 contributions) together total $426,750 — nearly triple Texas itself ($160,138.64, 125 contributions), with Maryland, Florida, and California rounding out the next tier.

## Major Spending

**Poolhouse Agency LLC** (Richmond, VA) is the single largest payee at **$408,243.00**, entirely booked as "Advertising Expenses — Media Strategy Consulting" across five buys ranging from $37,723 to $161,480, concentrated in January–February 2026 ahead of the March primary. **Chase - Card Member Services** is second at $380,106.51, booked as lump card payments under "Administrative/Salary/Overhead Expenses."

That Chase total isn't a black box: this filer's Schedule B never populates the `back_reference_transaction_id` field the tool normally uses to link memo-coded sub-transactions to their parent lump payment, so the analysis pulls the 870 memo-coded rows directly instead — $347,244.57 in real merchant-level detail. ## Takeaways

1. **A newly-collected joint fundraising committee reveals a distinct, tech-and-venture-capital donor network alongside the traditional defense-PAC money.** Judge Carter Victory Fund raised $712,076.61 this cycle, 93.9% of which ($669,173.08) reached Carter's own committees. Its top donors — Beacon AI, Silencer Shop, HavocAI, Riot Ventures, an employer listed simply as "Deterrence" — read like a defense-technology and venture-capital fundraising circuit, distinct from the legacy prime contractors (Lockheed, General Dynamics, BAE) giving directly to the campaign and PAC. Combined, the JFC's addition flips Carter's overall donor mix from PAC-majority (55.6% direct) to individual-majority (66.9% across all three committees).

2. **A separate joint fundraising committee touching this data, 2003 Victory Fund, mostly serves other members — not Carter.** Its own Schedule B shows only 13% of what it raised ($3,041.89 of $18,500.00 collected in the 2026 cycle) reaching Carter's committee; the bulk went to Mario Diaz-Balart, Mike Rogers, Tom Cole, and Mike Turner's campaigns. That's the textbook case this repository's CONNECTED-committee convention exists for — see Methodology for how it's handled here.

3. **The defense-and-aerospace donor bloc in the direct-committee data tracks the district's own new geography.** Fort Cavazos is one of the largest U.S. Army installations in the country, and 2026 redistricting brings the entire post into TX-31 for the first time, ending years of it being split with the neighboring 11th District. Lockheed Martin, General Dynamics, BAE Systems, Leonardo DRS, L3Harris, Textron, Boeing, Honeywell, and CACI together contributed roughly $126,000 directly — a closer fit to the district's constituent economy than most incumbents' donor rolls achieve.

4. **The leadership PAC hosted fundraisers at Austin FC's stadium.** Three "EVENT - VENUE RENTAL" payments totaling $40,509.54 to the city's Major League Soccer club, spread across July 2025 and June 2026, stand out among an otherwise conventional mix of ranch venues and the Capitol Hill Club.

5. **The card-payment "black box" wasn't actually opaque — it just needed a different key.** This candidate's FEC exports don't use the back-reference field the analysis tool normally relies on to unpack lump card payments, so pulling the 870 raw memo-coded rows directly was necessary to surface real spending on a private congressional club, airlines, pharmacies, grocery stores, and a consultant paid exclusively through card installments rather than direct checks.

6. **Personal finances reveal a concentrated stock position and a decades-old student loan.** Carter's 2025 Annual Financial Disclosure (filed 2026-05-14) shows his largest asset by far is a single Edward Jones brokerage account (valued $1,000,001–$5,000,000, generating $50,001–$100,000 in dividend income) built around Exxon Mobil common stock. His other disclosed income comes from two Texas public pensions, consistent with his pre-Congress career as a state district judge. On the liability side, alongside a mortgage refinanced with PNC Bank in 2024, he still lists a Mohela student loan dating to 1988.

- **A CONNECTED marker was added during this run.** A fourth committee's data, 2003 Victory Fund (C00905778), was found in `tx-31/john-carter/fec/` without a `CONNECTED` marker. Per gotcha 9 in `tooling/analyze-candidate.rb`'s header, that marker exists specifically for committees financially tied to a candidate but not one of their own — and this committee's own Schedule B (2026 cycle) shows only $3,041.89 of the $18,500.00 it raised reaching Carter's own committee, with the remainder going to Mario Diaz-Balart, Mike Rogers, Tom Cole, and Mike Turner's campaigns. That's the same pattern gotcha 9 documents for August Pfluger's American Battleground Fund — a multi-candidate JFC, not one of the candidate's own committees — so an empty `CONNECTED` file was added to `tx-31/john-carter/fec/C00905778/` before this run, excluding it from the donor/disbursement totals above the same way the tool already excludes any other CONNECTED-marked committee. Its own itemized data (raised-from and sent-to-Carter figures) is available via `analyze-candidate.rb`'s own CONNECTED COMMITTEES output but, per this repository's standing policy that connected-committee data is opt-in for a written report, is not narrated in the sections above beyond Takeaway 2 and this note. By contrast, Judge Carter Victory Fund (C00717272) was checked the same way and found to send 93.9% of what it raises to Carter's own two committees plus a further 2.6% to the NRCC (standard practice for a victory fund run alongside the national party) — no evidence of serving other candidates — so it was left unmarked and folded in normally.
- **How the headline totals avoid double-counting:** with a JFC now folded in as a normal committee, its own Schedule B disbursements include $688,038.75 in transfers to Carter's own campaign and PAC and to the NRCC — money that would otherwise be counted once as "the JFC's spending" and again when the campaign/PAC later spends the same dollars. `analyze-candidate.rb` already buckets these under their own `category_code_full` value, "Transfers," precisely because a JFC redistributing pooled proceeds isn't spending in the ads/staff/travel sense (a general note in the tool's header, not specific to this candidate). This report's $1,585,054.54 combined-spending figure is the sum of all three committees' itemized disbursements minus that $688,038.75 Transfers bucket; the $1,716,913.13 combined-receipts figure needs no equivalent adjustment, since each committee's own itemized Schedule A already excludes incoming "Transfers from authorized committees" from its own donor totals (gotcha 3), so the three committees' receipts represent three genuinely separate pools of outside money with no overlap.
